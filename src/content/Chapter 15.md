### The Shifting City
Iris woke up. It had been a long night. She had almost died the day before, and it was a relief she was able to get some rest. As she pushed herself out of the bed, something felt different. The air was heavy, damp, and carried the sharp tang of pulverized limestone and woodsmoke. Beneath her feet a low, continuous tectonic hum vibrated through the floorboards, a rhythmic pulsing that felt nothing like the steady ground she knew. As Iris stood up, she noticed the room was completely different. The rustic wood of her familiar tavern was gone, replaced by peeling gold leaf and high, vaulted ceilings. She hadn't been in that inn since last night.

Iris rushed towards the window. As she violently tugged on the curtains, trying to peel them apart to see the view, what she saw almost left her frozen in place. Stunned like a traveler staring into a sudden abyss, the view wasn't Askent Town. No, they were in a whole different city. Right outside, a humongous pillar of basaltic stone extruded from the earth. It loomed like the monolithic structures that lifted Askent Town out of the depths, but unlike the singular pillar, there were many, each carrying what seemed to be an array of palatial homes stacked precariously on its crest.

She glared longer, noticing how the ground wasn't level. Cracks were all around the city, where grand limestone and mossy brick boulevards were either cleanly severed, dropping fifty feet into misty, shadowed trenches or buckling upward into jagged stone plateaus. Moreover, Iris couldn't get over the towering Corinthian columns, marble caryatids, and ornate arched windows that clung stubbornly to the faces of the ruptured cliffs, trying desperately to impose classical beauty onto a geological nightmare.

Iris rushed into Nash's room, barging in without warning. Nash jolted upwards as Iris almost fell forwards, panting out half a sentence.

“Where are we?” Iris asked alarmingly. 

Nash scrambled up, realising where they were. “Bassecreuse…The city I told you about…”

Iris paused, still trying to figure everything out. Nothing would have prepared her for this city. It looked like an absolute pile of apocalypse. “I'm going to find Salem,”

“He's not here,” Nash replied coldly, as if he knew. “This is my home, there's only 2 rooms.”

Iris looked around, amazed at the interior. It looked much nicer than most places she had been, and the Beaux Art style definitely stood out. Intricate plaster moldings crawled along the edges of the high ceiling, mapping out delicate patterns of acanthus leaves and classical rosettes that had been meticulously brushed with gold leaf. Even the door was grand, flanked by miniature pilasters and handles lined with silver. 

“Why don't I take you around Bassecreuse? We can get you a new mech and blaster. I promise we'll go look for them after we get you geared up.” Nash began. Iris's eyes lit up and nodded with a smile, appreciating the gesture.

~

Before long, Iris found herself walking along the jagged paths of Bassecreuse with Nash guiding her. As they reached the intersection, a grand beheaded statue stood. The shoulders were chipped and a large crack had appeared near the legs, but no one seemed to care.

“That's what we call Wandjina,” Nash smiled, pointing at the decapitated head laying on the ledge above. Strangely, it looked connected from afar, but the disconnect just jarred the environment, creating the unsettling but grand atmosphere. 

“The mechanics shop is just up ahead. It sells all sorts of stuff!” Nash exclaimed, happily leading the way. As the pair stood in front of the exalted golden hued store, Iris was already eying one of the blasters that hung in on the windows.

The grip was textured with what seemed to be a smooth aluminium where a carbon fibre frame held the barrel. 

“You like what you see?” a voice smirked as Iris saw the shopkeeper gaze at her through the window. “Come on in!”

The store's exterior was inferior to its interior as the ceiling was lined with a faint neon yellow strip that radiated a soft ethereal glow down onto the displays. Iris inspected the blaster, feeling the barrel.

“The blades are made from a special alloy forged from the Golden Mines. The intellect of a mole fused palladium and copper— one of a kind.” the shopkeeper grinned. “Ain't even the best part! The handle is detachable. Attachments are at the back!”

Nash was already at the back, looking at the new Rose Gold Sniper Stock. Iris walked over, tapping Nash on shoulder. “Can I get that blaster?”

“Yeah, what do you think of this stock? This is Ridgeland technology, one of a kind…” Nash whispered, motioning towards the piece he was just looking at. Iris brushed her hand against the Rose Gold stock, inspecting it. It felt soft and delicate, but bizarre as it was confusing on how it worked. 

“How does this work?” Iris asked. The Shopkeeper slowly approached them, warmly helping Iris.

“Ey kid, you ain't around here?” he asked, grabbing the stock and attaching it onto the blaster Iris had eyed. The stock clicked, the display lighting up. “The latest model's got 5 modes, you just choose!”

As he pressed the button, the stock opened up, revealing a small tripod stand, muzzle and scope. The shopkeeper gracefully attached the components, turning the compact blaster into what seemed to be a fully fledged sniper. The front barrel and muzzle neatly slid in place beneath the blades, the colours creating a cascading complex of pinks. Iris was satisfied, but wasn't feeling the sniper.

“Woah…That's cool, but I'm not good with snipers,” Iris grinned awkwardly, declining the attachment.

The shopkeeper disassembled the sniper quickly before reaching from the top shelf a strange looking box. It was hexagonal, with a few corners rounded for an almost futuristic look. The fragile label already made Iris become curious of its contents while Nash watched eagerly. The shopkeeper tore the cardboard apart, revealing the exotic blaster. 

“This is a special blaster, since most fellas around here use snipers. These never sell well so we've tucked 'em away, in pristine condition,” the shopkeeper explained. 

“Wait, are those magnetic?” Nash interrupted, noticing the rounded grip. 

Iris felt the padded grips, sliding her fingers around the circular handle. It wasn't heavy, light to the touch. The blades were much flatter, raised not as much as the previous. 

“Same alloy, but built for closer ranges. This beast comes with a special fusion attachment, allowing certain slugs to harmonise their powers,” the shopkeeper added, patting the blade. Iris was very impressed, her fingers tracing the seam that stuck what seemed to be detachable blasters.

“So what's this seam? Will this blaster be taken apart?” Iris asked, prying the blaster apart.

The shopkeeper gently pushed a small button near the accelerator core, unlocking the mechanism that bound the two pistols together. “Yes, pure magnetics. When detached, you practically have two blasters, pretty cool right?” 

Iris's face lit up, extremely pleased with the weapon. “How much?” 

“Free of charge. To most Ridgelanders, close combat is like asking for death. If no one was going to take that blaster, they would've scrapped it soon,” the shopkeeper grumbled sarcastically. 

“Oh yeah, Raphael, she's also looking for a mech. You got any in stock?” Nash asked, remembering that their shopping list wasn't complete.

Raphael, the shopkeeper paused for a moment, then peered back through the backdoor before answering Nash. “Yeah, we don't have any stock. Though I did happen to get a broken mech yesterday, it seems like the Chasm slug is helping to keep me in business…”

“Another one? Mind if we have a look?” Nash asked, already having a fuzzy image in his head on which mech it was. 

“Yeah, follow me, though it's not finished. The engine's wrecked and the whole system is outdated,” Raphael explained, holding the door open. 

And before Iris knew it, she saw her own mech being suspended in the air, being tinkered with. “Wait…That's my mech…”

“Looks like the Chasm was busy…You're lucky you don't need a new mech…They cost a lot…” Nash grimaced, happy that the mech was being fixed.

“Oh, this piece of junk is yours?” Raphael questioned, hammering the bent leg off. 

“Yeah, there was an accident. Glad its back though. When do you reckon it will be finished?” Nash asked.

Raphael sighed, then checked the core, buried by layers of aluminium inside the mech's body. “Give or take, by the end of the day. I'd do it for ya friends.”

Iris wasn't sure on how to express her gratitude— Not to Raphael but to the Chasm slug, saving her mech. But just seeing that sly dingo faced mech was enough to get her grinning from ear to ear, excited for what was to come. 

“Alright, let's go!” Nash signalled, leading Iris towards the heart of Bassecreuse. As they stepped out of the shop, Iris suddenly froze. The store had been raised multiple metres up, as if the ground was shifting in real time. The pillar loomed high as Iris peered down, only seeing the disconnected footpath crash into the pillar's base. She gazed down at the city center, also being different from what she saw in the morning.

Her voice trembled like a flickering lamp, trying to understand Bassecreuse's true nature. “Is the city…moving?”

“Oh yeah, I forgot to mention it. Don't worry, though it may appear scary and unstable, the movements Wandjina controls, there is a pattern,” Nash assured. As Iris followed his trail, spectacular bridges and stairwells untucked themselves behind the pillars, gently reaching downwards. The railings were neatly perfected with intricate details, resembling the city's elegance. As Iris glided her palms down the railings, she could feel the magic, the essence of life that flooded Bassacreuse. It was different. It was calm, peaceful and controlled unlike the Jungle or the Desert. 

The path down the suspended walkway reached the ground, only for another chunk in front of them to be sunken down below the Earth. As Iris watched the bridge rise, the platforms began to stretch in a way that didn’t make any technical sense to her. The bridge from each section snapped together with a satisfying click, connecting the two stone pillars. 

Moments like walking down the main streets touched Iris deeply. Nash, like a guide, led Iris around the city, finding the most obscure locations in Bassecreuse. Small carved alleyways appeared etched into few stone pillars and others housed small stalls that were folded neatly to stay flush. It was like a micro-adventure with each turn, each corner having something fresh to encounter.

Merchants continued their trades. Children raced between plazas. Musicians played beneath archways that had likely stood somewhere else only hours before. The city moved, but its people moved with it.
Iris found herself staring less at the mechanisms of Wandjina and more at the people who called the Ridgelands home.

Every few hours, a distant rumble echoed through the cavern as another section of Bassacreuse shifted into place. Bridges folded outwards. Staircases rotated. Entire platforms drifted along invisible currents. 

Yet nobody panicked.

“So you’ve cracked Wandjina’s code?” Iris asked, sipping on the locally brewed beverages the city had to offer. Nash had welcomed Iris to one of his favourite cafes within the city, having a deserved rest in the mid-afternoon breeze. The rich taste of citrus fused with the pungent aroma of eucalyptus tea was enough to intoxinate Iris into loving the city in the strangest of ways, appreciating its distinct fabled aesthetic.

Nash smiled as he sat beside Iris, pondering on his own journeys outside of the Ridgelands. “Like I said earlier, it’s a pattern. We’ve mapped out the chunks that Wandjina moves, so we know not to build there. But it’s still unpredictable on how it moves. That’s why I’ve been travelling, at least. Perhaps there’s an answer…”

“Nonetheless, Bassecreuse is truly enchanting…” Iris began. As the delicate pastries were served on lavish tableware, the line between extravagance and disconnection began to fade. It was so nice to see how people were living in this city that moved on an hourly basis. It felt so informal of the Earth, but at the same time, the adaptability of human nature let Iris fall in love with the culture. “I wonder how Salem is doing…”

For the first time since arriving in the Ridgelands, she realised she missed him. It felt like her mind was like the Ridgelands itself, disconnected, fractured in a way.
