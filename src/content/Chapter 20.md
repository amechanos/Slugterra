### The Dreaming, Part 2

Iris meditated for a moment, embracing the solitude the Alpine Peaks gave her. The soft, yet cold breeze almost whispered to her, feeling a strange sense as she bathed under the winds. Her hair brushed her face as she gazed below, Verdud's silhouette on the horizon. 

“This world… The Ridgelands…” Iris began, her words barely audible.

“What?” Nash glanced at her worried expression.

Iris sighed, perhaps it was relief but to Nash, it just felt like disappointment, seeing his home have no meaning to Iris's journey. Nothing had changed. Just kids trying to seek for truth. But Iris didn't finish her sentence, leaving the tension on those words that began to build in Nash's mind.

“Iris…What is your purpose? Apart from being healed, why are you and Salem here? You're not Ridgelanders. There's no place for people like you here…” Nash asked. Though a strange question, Iris stared at him blankly for a moment without an answer.

Words forgot to seep out from the cracks in between her lips— nothing. But then in one breath, Iris blurted out the truth. “My father—” Her voice glitched like a broken signal, almost as if Nash had triggered something within her. “I'm looking for him…”

But something within Iris burned. Nash's words lingered longer than they should have. No place. The Desert wasn't home anymore. The Ridgelands never had been. For the first time, Iris wondered if she was following Salem because she wanted answers. Or perhaps  because it was that she had in fact, nowhere else to go.

“Nash, c'mon,” Iris snapped back, almost as if she was daydreaming for a moment. She was, her mind still cast upon her father. The role of the Watcher still lingered deep. “We have to hurry to the Deep Roots…I know it's not my duty, but I want to help.”

\~

Nash and Iris rushed towards the Deep Roots. The forest cleared and gave way to the familiar tree-like canyon. Ravines branched out and the canyon stood like the roots of the tree, holding the abomination together. The Alpine Peaks were far gone, but the peace had carried over. The faint sound of Hoverbugs sweeping over the airscape faded, where rubble cracked off the cliffs of the hanging canyon.

The route to the Deep Roots hadn't changed. But to Iris, it had. Seeing the path down sent shivers down her body. The wall was so close, Iris finally realised the proximity of the wall. As the duo turned the corner towards the entrance, a familiar whistling hummed.

“I presumed you'd finally arrive, Nash,” Nole smirked. “What have you two been up to?”

“None of your business. This needs to end, now.” Nash exclaimed. 

The emphasis disturbed Nole, his tone snapping back. “You and fighting Wandjina— Are you going to do this or not? Was your expedition to the desert all for nothing?”

“No\! I got Iris…and Salem…” 

“And? You’re just using them\! You promised me you would save the Ridgelands\! What apart from brainwashing and exploitation have you done with Iris, huh? Are they pawns to your research?”

Iris gasped, unsure what even was unfolding. But for once, she understood Nole's frustration. He was right. Nash wasn't doing the right thing either. Iris felt used like a pawn in Nash's grand plan to save the Ridgelands but had failed.

“No…Iris…Don’t get it wrong…” Nash’s tone declined. Iris had no remorse.

“You humans are wrong\! While you live your lives, us moles are trying to restore our lives\!” Nole shouted, clearly frustrated at Nash. His voice hissed like a dirty viper.  “Dirty traitor…”

“But…isn’t that what makes us human? What’s your next challenge once you overcome Wandjina? What do you live for?” Salem pondered. Nash and Iris unmounted their mechs, making the face to face confrontation feel more personal than ever.

“Don’t throw philosophical remedies at me, I’m done here. I’m going back to Verdud,” Nole announced, storming out of the conversation. “Shrue's almost done with his plan. It will only be a matter of time before us superior moles put an end to Wandjina\!”

Nash had never seen Nole this tempered, but he knew it was a topic that deeply resented with him. But either way, Salem, Nash and Iris couldn’t let Shrue complete his plan. Dark Water was evil enough, and cursing the Ridgelands would have devastating effects, more than just destroying Wandjina completely.

“Nole\!” Nash shouted. “Wandjina isn’t the problem…It’s us…” 

Nole's expression faded, an impulsive demonic one taking over. He felt insulted. 

“Wandjina isn't what we think it is\! Our distinction— Mole and Human— We're so apart…So far apart that–”

Before Nash could finish his sentence, out of pure anger and fury, Nole couldn't resist his blinding loyalty. Nole's Thresher cannonballed towards Nash, its blades ripping into his arm as his body launched into the rocky walls. He cried out in pain, where Wandjina reacted with every struggle. Finally, the Thresher returned to protoform, leaving a deep wound in Nash's dominant right arm.

“Nash\!” Iris screamed, rushing to his side without a thought. Salem held his blaster up, aimed at Nole but unable to push the trigger. “Oh my god…”

“Apart? You have done nothing for the better\!” Nole spat, walking away like nothing had happened. Iris rushed towards Nash, covering the wound with a cloth. Salem, disgraced, aided Nash together, helping him up as the three knew that something terrible was brewing. 

“No…Iris…Go for Shrue\! Let Salem help…” Nash weakly groaned. His heartbeat raced uncontrollably, the two scared of what was going to happen next.

“Are we seriously just letting Nole walk away? After what he just did?” Salem asked as he opened the first aid kit. He could hear Nash's heavy breaths, the profuse exhales shaking Salem's own movements. His hands also shook for no reason, terrified of nothing. The bandages almost slipped out his hand, but Iris helped, not leaving their side.

“What are you doing? Wandjina's going to die…Iris…You need to go…” Nash muttered.

“No. We have time.” Iris exclaimed. “And we'll do it together.”
