### The City of Cubes
Askent town rose to the birth of light within the overhead Solaryte crystals. The warm light beamed down, highlighting the city with a caring grasp. Jagged lines of environment came into view as the morning’s breath sifted through the room. Salem rubbed his eyes, collecting his thoughts of the day before. Everything still felt sudden, particularly with the fall. 

A gleeful hum echoed outside his room. It sounded like Nole, already up and about. Salem stared outside the window, taking a deep breath. 

“Ah…It still feels…Different…” 

Salem still couldn’t wrap his head around Wandjina. How could people even live with this force? As he finally stood up and got dressed, Nole’s whistling walked down the halls, almost as if he was waiting for someone. Salem pushed his door open, only to find the halls to be dead silent. Salem continued forwards, heading for Iris’s room.

Salem knocked. No sound. He knocked again with no response.

“Iris?” Salem exclaimed, pushing the door open. Iris wasn't there. The curtains weren’t opened, still drawn shut with the sheets neatly tucked in on the sides. It was as if no one had been in the room.

“Pffft, a bunch of losers. They probably woke up earlier,” Nole yawned, stretching. Salem turned to see Nole somehow appear out of nowhere, almost as if he was lurking in the shadows somehow. 

Salem glanced at the clock that read 09:13. “Where would they have gone that early?” 

Nole snickered, leaning against the doorway. “Hmph, I don't know. I mean, whatever the case may be, it is just you and me.”

Salem sighed as he went to undo the curtains, revealing the cool morning breeze that drifted through Askent Town. As the sunlight finally flooded the quiet room, Salem brought himself to Iris's bed, sitting on the edge. Nole jumped beside him, gazing out as if he was also surprised, knowing well he had seen the view hundreds of times. Mushroom capped Fungaroo and armored Speedstinger slugs hopped from porch to porch, setting the quiet but bustling morning of Askent Town. For some reason, the longer Salem stared outside, the steeper the town felt as if the pillar rooted into the abyss was rotating.

The morning felt like hours. Salem had dragged himself down to the inn's eatery, grabbing local breakfast. A lush variety of pastries were lined on the trays, with colorful jams spread beside them. Nole had forked a plate full of bacon and worms. 

“So, what do you think, Salem?” Nole asked, chomping on his food.

“Well, the Ridgelands is alright…” Salem smiled, taking a small bite from the sweet pastry. He gazed outside the radiant little windows, watching as locals wandered the tilted streets of Askent.

~

“Where are we going?” Salem asked as the duo left the inn. 

Nole smiled, mounting his mech. “Verdud! My home. Might as well have a quick trip back to resupply!”

“Verdud?” Salem questioned, confused by the strange name. It wasn't like anything he had heard of before, capturing a distinct, brutal undertone. Salem's initial thoughts had been jumbled around already, where Nole stared at him confusedly.

“Eh? I mean we don't know where Iris and Nash went, so unless you want to get lost, you have to follow me,” Nole explained formally, holding a stern tone. Salem sighed, following Nole's lead as they began to ride towards the Southern exit which had tilted down to give way to the surrounding land. 

The intricate drawbridge lowered, seamlessly connecting the exit to the land where it was relatively flat with minimal incline. Their mechs trotted along, landing on the grassy green plains that stood in front of them. As they travelled, the landscape shifted, more plants began to appear on both sides of the main path that wound down the valleys. Boreal stemmed mushrooms lined the path neatly, overshadowed by stretching trees that seemed to cast dancing shadows onto the path. The terrain felt thicker in vegetation, denser with a heavy muddy stench that felt like the insides of the Earth was folded outwards.

New slugs joined the quiet ride, hopping from cap to cap, branch to branch. Designed in a pale, muted brown, the slick Fungaroo slugs hopped ecstatically, playing with the environment. Some bounced higher than where the trees reached while others performed flips midair, landing perfectly each time.

“Those guys look fun…” Salem muttered. His Tenasher cheered from its shell, barely loud enough to turn the heads of a few Fungaroos that were close by.

“Fungaroos are probably the most sly slugs you'll ever meet…They bounce like springs so fast!” Nole exclaimed as the two rode along in their mechs along the jagged path. “Although their innocent looking mushroom cap does look cute, don't take your chances!”

Salem grinned, watching the playful slugs dance along the shrubs that lined the path. It reminded him of home, the Jungle where similar slugs hung out in the local flora, bringing joy to those who travelled nearby. But minute by minute, Nole's light explanation became a rant, a lecture of Ridgeland life that Salem couldn't bear to listen to. He wanted to experience the environment himself. Through Nole's explanation, Salem felt less immersed and more disconnected than usual. The uneasy sense that bored his mind out.

“Like, the slugs— can't they just ask Wandjina to stop? Isn't a spirit with slug energy?” 

Salem was too unfocused, on autopilot as he absorbed the environment around him. Gentle breeze. Not a single word, only a soft exhale left Salem's body. His eyes rested, feeling the Ridgelands caressing his body like a nurturing god. It felt strange. 

“Are you even listening?” Nole asked, trying to gather Salem's attention back.

“Sorry, just blanked out for a moment. Continue…” Salem wasn’t feeling right, unfocused and slow. Perhaps it was Iris’s absence and the thought of something sinister taking force. It felt like the Jungle again. Salem didn’t trust Wadnjina. To him, it was another illusion like the Rainbow Serpent.

“As I was saying, we're almost at Verdud!” Nole exclaimed cheerfully, motioning at the grand scale of the city. Though it felt short lived as just over the horizon, a grid-like array of spherical domes covered the ground. It didn’t feel large or techy, rather a lackluster settlement that initially suggested the incompetence of such a race. These domes, decently sized, felt like circular windows that eyed the cavern floor. Salem was astonished. Suddenly, right in front of their eyes, the ground rumbled, a chunk rising up from the Earth. 

Wandjina. 

The massive pillar revealed its lengthy body, where Salem only saw tunnels that burrowed outwards of the pillar. It felt like the pillar was segmented, with distinct cube-like shapes that defined the architecture of Verdud. 

“Woah…” Salem was frozen in awe, gazing at the towering pillars that housed what seemed to be rooms or fully functional locations. The more he observed, the finer details came into view. These tunnels weren't just regular tunnels the moles had dug, they seemed weathered, as if the same tunnel were dug and then patched in cycles. A gentle zephyr of mushroom-scented air wafted across the palatial city, diffusing a rich umami aroma. Nole took a deep breath in then happily exhaled a sense of glee, riding forwards. As they parked their mechs against a nearby dome, Salem traced his fingers on the glass dome, gazing down into what seemed to be a home. 

“This is my home!” Nole grinned.

“How do you know? This place is like a maze!” Salem exclaimed.

Nole chuckled, opening the door that led them inside the dome. “I don't! I guess, then find my way home!” 

The interior of the dome was a dizzying cathedral of negative space, where Solaryte filtered poorly through thick, translucent mineral panes to cast a dim, amber glow over massive vertical shafts. As they entered the glass room, a small device protruded from the ground, displaying the nodes that were connected vertically. Nole swiftly configured the device, checking what was below. Salem hitched behind Nole, peeking at the display's contents that revealed the lab on level -3.

“Aha! The Lab!” Nole exclaimed, preparing to slide down the tunnel. 

Holding concern, Salem hesitated, unsure about following Nole. “Are you sure it's safe?”

“Of course!” Nole rolled his eyes, strolling forwards casually. Salem followed carefully, letting his body slowly travel down the narrow tunnel. The tunnel, narrow and bumpy, was rather uncomfortable for Salem as he slid down, realising the nature of the tunnels. They weren’t necessarily designed for humans. As the tunnel ended, Salem found the organic curves of the surface dome to vanish, being replaced by a brutal, geometric interior lined with thick stone and concrete. In fact, the room they entered was rather spacious, contrasting Salem’s beliefs of the city to be a claustrophobic mess buried in a network system underground. Standing on the mesh catwalk and looking down to the lower level of the room, Nole waved at other moles that casually lounged around. To Salem, it wasn’t anything like home or the Ridgelands above, where the geometric precision of the city began to hit him with a crushing sense of vertigo.

“Down one more!” Nole instructed, walking down the stairway towards what seemed to be another tunnel. 

Moving down, the lab gave way to itself, revealing a similar architecture to the previous node. It wasn't overly decorated, still minimalist at best with cyber aesthetic lights that lined the ceiling. It all felt strange, a civilisation trying to perfect the change. Ladders and hollowed out tunnels connected cube-like chunks within the maze as the two traversed the underground citadel. Monotone greys lined the walls, hiding the icky brown dirt that surrounded the chunk. Salem paid close attention to its architecture, impressed by the technology. Slick titanium white tiles paved the floors as the pair walked into the laboratory.

“Ah, Nole! Welcome back my good friend!” a voice chirped. Nole smiled at the sight of the nerdy mole that approached him, dressed in a clean lab coat.

“Ah, Shrue!” Nole grinned, exchanging a hearty handshake. “Salem, I would like to introduce you to one of the leading scientists here, Shrue Taleppe!” 

“Hi, my name is Salem,” Salem smiled awkwardly, reaching down low to meet the mole's grasp. “This place feels so futuristic!”

Shrue chuckled. “Futuristic?”

Shrue glanced around while Nole coughed sarcastically, dampening the mystical weight that held within Salem's expectations.

“This is nowhere near futuristic. Our home is constantly displaced by Wandjina's movements. It destroys our work— the tunnels that we might dig one day, gone the next. Here, we are researching ways to prevent Wandjina from acting!” Shrue explained, pointing to a display that had magically appeared out of thin air. Salem stood in shock as he tried to understand the technology behind the device, finally recognising a line of hologram projector bolted to the floor. 

The translucent display projected the 3D map of the city, tracking each node as Wandjina slowly moved. Salem stepped forward, interacting with the screen. The pillar they were on focused, then began to beam up. It rendered itself into a three dimensional space where Salem could see each room the pillar had stored.

“So, what’s the proposed plan?” Salem asked. His mind had begun to sink into the tapestry of thoughts and dreams of the people around him, realising Wandjina’s threat. He saw what it did to Askent town, making it steeper. Nole had explained that the city they were in was constantly moving. If anything, Salem knew that change could never go on forever. It would break civilization.

The screen flickered white before transitioning to a different page, highlighting a vial with an unknown substance. 

“I’m working on a serum that can nullify Wandjina’s effects. I’ve begun testing, but I haven’t finalised anything,” Shrue explained, the diagram flashing. Labels casted themselves from the center image, breaking down everything about the serum and how it was processed, signalling the needed trust Salem wanted to see. The cracks had begun to seal themselves shut in Salem’s mind, now immersed with the moles’ ideology. 

Whilst the temperance of the environment remained futuristic, Nole's cheerful tone heavily dimmed the silence, gladly emphasising his eagerness. “How can we help?” 

“Help?” Shrue began, stroking his chin. “I suppose we could head to Midnight Cavern. It's rumoured that the last component is there…” 

As words were exchanged, the lab had moved, shifted across and up displacing them once again. Then, almost immediately, a line of fatigued moles filed out from what seemed to be a break room. Fresh dirt coated their fur and uniforms. Some carried surveying equipment while others dragged bundles of cable. Holding heavy eye bags and slouched postures, there was no doubt that they had been constantly at work, perhaps rerouting the tunnels all night whenever Wandjina shifted. Salem watched cautiously, rather shocked at the conditions in Verdud.

 “That's…not normal?” Salem asked, evidently shaken. The sound of dirt being dug shrivelled underground, creating new tunnels that led to other nodes.

"That's the morning crew. The night crew's still digging.” Nole replied.

“Isn't there technology to dig?” Salem added. 

“Yes, but it's lost to Earth. We've tried several times, but it's like Wandjina eats it…” Shrue explained. “We have to get a move on if we want to end this…” 

The fractured city, like a Rubik's cube turning away, was all that Salem could think about. As they exited the labyrinth-like city, more pillars of cubes had either sunk or rose above the ground. 

“Well, what are we waiting for? Let's go and fix this world.” Shrue exclaimed. Salem glanced back as they began riding towards Wandjina, reminiscing of the culture in Verdud. Such a strange place. But what place wasn't strange? Life just felt fragmented in the Ridgelands. And there it was, Wandjina in action. Nothing was still for a moment in Verdud.
