### Dreams of a Desert

The Boundless Barrier Wall loomed over the horizon like a silent, obsidian god, joining bluntly against the jagged cavern ceiling. It was now within reach. Iris stood at the mouth of the canyon, admiring the wall in all of its beauty. Were there monsters on the other side?

The arid desert wind chaotically whipped at her umber hair as she charted the path ahead. Her blue eyes fixed on the chaotic sprawl of brass and iron that burst from the base of the wall like a mechanical tumor. She was close. She was close, yet so far. Her mecha’s legs trotted along the now quiet dunes, much calmer than the eye of the desert where the constant winds splashed sunkissed sand upon her. Finally arriving soon after, there was an unsettling atmosphere that masked the entrance, something that told Iris it was far more bizarre than what she first assumed it to be. It was a rather mysterious entrance, framed with rotting wooden planks that seemed to spiral deep underground. It felt like a way to go beyond the wall, but also a way that would lead to potential chaos. Unsaddling her mech and slowly walking towards the entrance, there were more dystopian elements that reinforced the abandoned nature of this place. The landmark's name, etched on the wooden boards had almost faded completely, with only the words “The Ducts” eligible. Wasted pickaxes and shovels littered the entrance alongside rusted, opened crates sitting beside what seemed to once be shelves. The air around The Ducts vibrated with a low, sub-harmonic hum, the kind of sound that felt less like a noise and more like a headache. As Iris parked her mech and stood worried at the entrance, the sand bashed against the rusted metal plates that were beneath her, echoing a vibration through the soles of her feet that told of decades of neglect.

\*“The Ducts, eh? Looks like we're getting close. Don't worry dad, I'll find you…”\*

As she looked around, hundreds of pipes that seeped into the mines surrounded her vision. The deeper she went, the more there was. Some were as thin as a finger, others wide enough to drive a carriage through. Some wriggled out of the mouth of the mine entrance and some seemed to coil around jagged rock formations. There were even some that thrusted back into the sand as if they were seeking something, like fuel deep within the ground. It very much reminded Iris of the architecture seen in Steamvale, where the industrial feel was intense and gritty. As she carefully made her way deeper into the throat of the mine, the searing desert heat vanished, replaced by a damp, heavy chill that clung to her skin. It darkened exponentially, forcing Iris to take out her flashlight where it beamed a small spot of guidance ahead. Iris kept forward, using the small spot to uncover the secrets of the walls within The Ducts, ensuring she didn't miss anything important. She was there to explore and seek out answers afterall. The scent of the desert was slowly replaced by a much cooler and pungent tang of oxidized copper as Iris continued on her dangerously quiet path. Every breath Iris took left a faint, metallic taste on the back of her tongue, a flavor like sucking on a copper coin. Each step haunted her body, surging blood up into her head making her think entities were pursuing her. It felt more like a nightmare the deeper she went. The transition was jarring enough to make Iris lean against the far left wall, clutching onto it for dear life. If she ever was scared, she knew holding the wall could only help her balance and stabilise her if she were to engage in a battle. She hoped this wasn't the case. Iris didn't want to fight, especially in this environment.

As she ventured deeper, the structural integrity of the mine seemed to decay, withering that of the fully intact pipes. Some began to suddenly cut off, others split open. Iris traced the path of a thick pipe that managed to survive the deteriorating environment, slightly vibrating to the hum of The Ducts as if there was a powered engine plugged inside it. She pressed her palm against it harder. Her hands came off of it damp and slightly slimy. Again, it felt more nightmarish the more she stayed.

"This feels…too wet for a desert…" she muttered as her eyes fluttered wider. The sense of fear crept deeper within her. “The architecture as well…”

Iris nervously studied the way the passage ahead curved. That mint-rain scent intensified. Something was off. As her torchlight unmasked the intricately carved ceiling, Iris noticed the strange, almost archaic engravings along the topsides of the walls. It was in fact ancient infrastructure after a few seconds of processing. What was really The Ducts? Was someone trying to hide this beneath layers of abandonment and decay? Even so, why? It only added to the ominous undertone to The Ducts, reinforcing the unknown nature of the ever-angry Drunken Desert.

For once, the sound of water droplets echoed.

The fat sound, accompanied by the reverb supported by The Duct's labyrinth-like structure muffled Iris's senses. It ensnared her like fish out of water, slowing her pace and actions down significantly. The environment was playing games upon Iris's behalf, manipulating her fear in the worst of ways.

Then, there was the sound of ancient, congealed machine oil dripping.

Periodic and echoed, it harmonised with the constant whirr of cooling. She didn’t want to admit her fear of the path ahead as it was mostly dark, only illuminated by the weak shine of the torch she held.

*“Is it just me, or is it getting colder?”*

Up ahead, there was a steep drop down to a lower level. There seemed to be a deeper layer submerged in what looked like water, though its appearance was far from life-giving. The liquid was a thick, murky green, shimmering with an unnatural luminescence that suggested an acidic, deadly potency. The mine no longer looked like a mine as the tunnel forward appeared as a trench filled with the liquid, with the overhead section slightly hollowed out. Trailing along the solid edges of the green river, Iris took a moment to analyse the area. The air here was indeed cooler, plagued by a damp scent of rain and mint. The silence of the cavernous room was heavy, broken only by the sluggish churning of the green river below. It didn't feel like a desert anymore, it felt as if she was stuck in an underground Jungle Temple.

But the heat of the desert didn't fade. Something had kept burning inside Iris, her heart still pumping as if she was in the desert. It beat fast to match the pace of the desert, aligning speed to strength. Still half dazzled by the cool satisfaction, a bizarre sound emerged from down the river. Then, the rhythm of the cavern shifted. It felt like a sound, a human one where the water splashed without sync, disturbing the flow of the mine's eerie ambience. Iris froze, with her body still radiating the heat of the desert. She was excessively sweating, even when a mysterious breeze caught her hair again this deep within the mines. Just what was this place?

Iris stared into the glow until her eyes ached, trying to make the haze confess what it was hiding. The trench stretched ahead with scatterings of overgrown vines embedded into the fading concrete wall, with the stone bricks still blurred and out of sight ahead. 

Squinting through the shifting green haze ever so frightened, Iris's blue eyes locked onto a movement in the distance. A faint, dark and real silhouette emerged from the emerald fog. The figure moved with a strange, rhythmic gait, its shadow stretching long and distorted. Iris crept ever so quietly, unwilling to alert the strange entity. It was unclear whether it was a monster or another person, but Iris knew she had to stay on high alert. More shadows crept along the base of the walls, now lurking amongst the tunnel. Iris focused her torchlight ahead, trying to make clear of exactly what it was. 

There was a loud click before the shadows faded, engulfing the path forward in utter darkness. Iris's torch had died, trapping her in darkness surrounded with the possibility of monsters. The silhouette had disappeared out of sight.

*“Danggit\!”* Iris hissed, thinking out loud. Without her torch, it was going to be brutal to navigate the rest of The Ducts. She considered turning back, but she realised she was already too deep in to give up.

The longer she thought about it and stared forwards into the darkness, the more nauseating it felt. The strange, heavy pressure that weighed on her chest was only growing heavier, slowly accelerating her heartbeat. All there was to illuminate the path forward was the luminescent neon green river. It was still bubbling, casting a faint, ethereal green glow that was just enough to see the foot paths beside. Iris stepped forward in caution, trying to minimise the sound she was making to not alert any of the so-called monsters. She was afraid.

Her fingers slipped around the grip of her blaster priming her hand if she needed to. Then the river suddenly faded in colour, dimming its green radiance. It was sudden, causing Iris to lose focus on the target and her steady grip. The water clouded back into a dark, almost opaque black-blue, swallowing and snatching the entirety of the cavern of its light. No longer illuminated, the river revealed its truth: the green toxicity before was never toxic, it was an illusion and the liquid was rather just water. 

*“Ummm, is this supposed to happen?…”* Iris trembled. Iris was now officially trapped in darkness. She could not see a thing except for a slight glint off her blaster, reflecting something that was in the water. Iris panicked as she chaotically tried to feel her way forward, making sure she didn't fall into the river. Yes, though it was just water, Iris couldn't think about the fact on how the atmosphere of the environment had suddenly turned this cold, sending shivers throughout this body even though she was still \*in\* the desert.

The sound of water splashing grew. A bitter taste of the tensing air bled into Iris's mouth. This was it. Whatever the entity was, it was only getting closer.

“Fin?” Iris whispered, hoping to hear an answer. Her hoverbug didn't whimper a noise, clinging tight to her neck, also terrified. Iris knew what it was. Without a doubt, she knew she wasn't alone in the bowels of The Ducts. “Maybe Chuck was right…There…There are monsters out there…”

Her fingers clamped onto the chilling stone wall, trying to navigate her way through the dark backwards to where she came from. Iris's movements were rushed, laced in panic where her grip loosened from the excessive amount of sweat emitted. Her breath was now clear and loud, her adrenaline and heart beat all increasing. The secret pressure multiplied again. Iris could barely hold on.

“You lost?” a voice called. Iris jolted back, then a sudden luminance of a neon lime shattered the deafening darkness. “Don't worry, it's just a Glowbyss slug. They chill here in the depths of the Temple of the Lawa,”

Iris, still recoiling from the Glowbyss flashbang, slowly regained her vision. Now that the tunnel was fully lit thanks to the Glowbyss, Iris was finally able to see the true nature of the bottom layer of the Ducts. What was this ‘Temple of the Lawa’ this person was talking about?  The now greenly lit space was hauntingly futuristic, with the Glowbyss staring confused at the flinched Iris. The bright lights lined the tunnel, revealing the rather clean river that ran through the middle. No, it was never green, it was only the Glowbyss's colour that muddied the water. The green, clouded liquid she had previously seen was all an illusion, making Iris question the reality of The Ducts. Was this the truth?

“Who are you?” Iris suddenly exclaimed, loading her Armashelt and aiming her blaster to the boy's face. Her voice was shaky and her grip was unstable. The blaster shook with hundreds of micro-adjustments, signalling to the boy that she was terrified. 

“Calm down\!” The white haired boy now revealing himself was confused, attempting to deescalate the issue at hand. He began to reach for his rifle equipped on his back, loading a strange, green and gold slug into the barrel.

“I wouldn't draw blasters if I were you…Let's just say, you're too old fashioned…” the boy replied. His round spectacles shimmered the Glowbyss's lime light back at Iris again, fogging her vision. Though hypocritical in attempting to calm Iris down, the boy was also in a sense of danger and panic, with his instincts of facing anyone with a blaster was to equip his own.

“Try me you monster\!” Iris yelled, slinging her Armashelt straight towards the boy. It was second-nature for the boy to release the trigger, launching the mystical Gyrophyll slug towards the armoured Armashelt. The Gyrophyll, body laced in an intricate emerald coloured scale flew slow, as if it was moving in slow motion. Iris had high hopes to win the matchup, if only she had known what exactly she was up against.

As the Armashelt lunged towards the Gyrophyll, it struck its claw. Iris expected it to be a devastating blow, instantly crushing the Gyrophyll. But it wasn't. Instead, the Gyrophyll quickly hurled up into a ball-like shape right before the claw's impact, where it used the strike to generate an insanely high rotational speed, instantly propelling itself in a strange trajectory with the momentum the Armashelt had given it. The Gyrophyll, though knocked backwards, spun like a beyblade, rerouting back forwards and ripping through the air, knocking the Armashelt out instantly. It was headed towards Iris next, and she knew it was bad news.

“Time's up\!” the boy called out as he correctly calculated Gyrophyll's path, its speed falling to a mere 0.1km/h and stopping right before hitting Iris. Too stunned to speak, Iris was motionless, still processing what had just happened.

But still, within that moment, perhaps it was still the immense build of heat spiralling into chaos within her body, pulsating her heart to unprecedented speeds, she passed out cold. Now slumped against the wall, her feet dangled into the quiet stream. And again, there was silence.
