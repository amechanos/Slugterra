### Jaded Secrets

The jungle sang quietly, its dormant leaves and branches flexing in tune with the rhythmic winds that swept through the subterranean heights. It was a world of vibration and dampness. Iris reached out, her fingertips grazing the translucent, rubbery skin of the creatures clinging to the nearby stalks. They felt cool, almost like living gel, reacting to her touch with a faint, rhythmic pulse. The humidity in the cavern was a physical weight, a heavy blanket that pressed against Iris as she followed Salem deeper into the overgrowth. Every breath was thick; the air tasted of wet stone and ancient growth, a sharp contrast to the parched, dusty winds of the desert she had left behind.

As they moved, the local wildlife began to stir. Slugs slowly poked their heads out of the nearby vegetation, crouching on broad leaves and waiting patiently at the sight of Salem and the foreign girl. Their eyes, like tiny obsidian beads, tracked Iris’s every movement with a strange, unnerving intelligence. 

Mandimesh slugs slept stretched out, their flexible mesh-bodies bent to form living hammocks between the branches, while Vinedrill slugs perched like sentinels near stumps and stems. Nearby, Chrolobites happily chewed on leaves, their mandibles making a soft, rhythmic clicking, while Flaringoes chirped statically behind the thick bushes. 

The static noise hummed in Iris’s ears, a low-frequency vibration that seemed to travel through the thick air and settle in her bones. Salem paused, sweeping his arm across the horizon of the internal forest with a sense of guarded pride.

“This is the Jade Garden,” Salem motioned. Located just west of Rainbow Falls Cavern, the sight was enough to make Iris’s eyes wander like lost explorers, marvelling at the striking environment. The garden spoke of enchantment, radiating a thick, velvet atmosphere that felt like a caress against her skin. There was an ancient, overwhelming scent of crushed mint, choking the rough rocky ground so potently that Iris could actually taste the minerals on her tongue. A gentle pacific breeze, seemingly impossible this far underground, tugged against the overhead vines that spiralled deep into the jungle, resting under the distant cavern ceiling. Before her eyes, numerous colours slowly began to reveal themselves through the emerald gloom. She saw luscious fruits and vegetables growing on expansive farmland and orchards that the garden housed. The vegetation was stunning, and Iris felt a tug of spiritual awe. She knew instinctively that this garden was blessed somehow.  
“Our legendary farmers, Keoni, Lihara, and Tavira work hard here to provide excellent ingredients.”

“Woah…” Iris gasped, her voice barely a whisper as she leaned in to see a cluster of glowing berries. “This place… it’s so enchanting…”

“Yeah, I know, right? The farmers believe it’s the Rainbow Serpent’s doing. It is our god, after all”. Salem smiled, but the expression didn't quite reach his eyes. His gaze lingered on the heavy vines a second too long, as if searching for a fraying thread in a masterpiece.

As Salem guided Iris deeper, walking through neatly ploughed fields, Iris noticed the significant build-up of water sitting in crevices and cracks, evident of a flood. The large farmhouse was up ahead, standing isolated in the centre of the cavern. As they approached, the million translucent leaves that had framed their path slowly faded out of view as the cavern opened up. The space bathed her in a shimmering, sea-foam glow that made the very air seem liquid, as if they were walking along the bottom of a shallow, sunlit bay.

“So why are we here?” Iris questioned, her senses still struggling to adapt to the serene yet heavy environment. The floor, soaked in water, was rather slippery for her liking, and she nearly lost her footing.

“Because this is an isolated cavern. I need to inform my superiors about completing my task,” Salem explained, his tone turning professional. “Like I said, foreigners aren't welcome here. You'll be safe here for now. The farmers are still away waiting for the garden to recover from the flood”. 

“But I don't get it,” Iris interrupted. Her mind drew back to the Rainbow Serpent encounter, rather puzzled by the fact that they had to suppress it.

“Get what?” Salem asked. His tone, riddled in confusion, only deepened Iris's interpretation of the dark underbelly of the jungle.

“The serpent…If it's your *god*, why did we need to suppress it?” 

Salem sighed heavily as he gazed at the puddle at his feet. “You see, it's just been our way of telling the serpent that we don't need more resources. Slugs communicate better than us. I’ll be back soon”.

Salem swiftly slipped away, leaving Iris alone in the heart of the Jade Garden. Salem wasn't evil, but nor did he tell the truth and that was what made Iris to have an extremely neutral outlook on Salem. He wasn't an ordinary hunter. He wasn't just another boy supporting his tribe. There had to be a deeper meaning. The silence that followed was deafening. Without Salem's presence, the liquid air felt heavier, more suffocating, now that she was the only one breathing it. Despite the isolation, a small spark of hope flickered in her chest. She still couldn't believe there was a completely different world beyond the desert. The existence of such life made her think that perhaps, maybe, her father was still alive somewhere in this vast green labyrinth.

Seeking a distraction from her thoughts of the jungle being rather complex, Iris began to ponder around the farmland. She approached an enclosed den where strange, yellow fruits hung from low-hanging branches. Her fingers brushed against the waxy skin of one fruit; it felt cool and solid, like polished stone. It had a strong, piercing aroma— something like a lemon but more mystical, a scent that teased her memory but remained tantalizingly out of reach.

“I wish I had my mech…” she thought, dreaming of the uses the AI dashboard would provide for analyzing this flora. Without it, she had to rely on her senses. As she carefully inspected each fruit, she noticed that each surface was a different texture of life; some felt firm, others felt rough and grippy, while some felt so soft and mushy that two fingers could squeeze the juice out of it. The variety of exotic citrus and chili plants were absolutely magnificent, yet something felt wrong. Whatever it was, it couldn't leave her mind. The ever churning idea of an evil force lurking under the jungle's canopy was inescapable. In fact, for Iris, the fruit seemed too perfect, too uniform. She knelt to inspect the soil, looking for signs of the irrigation systems Keoni Raal, the farmhand responsible for the soil’s vitality, would have maintained. Instead of technical tools, her hand brushed against something papery. She found a scrap of paper half-buried in the damp mulch. It was somehow still intact despite the moisture. The handwriting was hurried, the ink bleeding into the yellowed paper as if written in a panic.

*“That damned Hexlet has decreased our annual harvest again\! The bad luck slug has ruined our harvest by making our crops wilt”.*

Iris read the words confidently but frowned as she looked at the vibrant plants around her. Nothing looked wilted. If anything, the garden was advancing with a predatory speed, its vines thick, muscular, and pulsing with a life that felt almost aggressive. The notes had an ominous undertone, filling Iris with the needed curiosity to question further. Driven by a growing unease, she moved toward the edge of the cavern to explore further. The ground here was different. It was less like a garden and more like a graveyard for the sea. Tangled, saltwater-stained ropes lay coiled like sleeping snakes. As she stepped over a pile of rot-choked mesh, the toe of her boot caught on a metallic glint. She reached down, pulling a rusted gutting knife from the silt, its blade notched and dull. Nearby, a discarded fishing net was fused into the dirt, as if the earth were trying to digest the synthetic fibers. Another corner of a note, covered in dirt and grime, protruded from beneath the net. Iris pulled it free, the smell of salt and stagnant water clinging to it.

*“The feast is tomorrow. We had our fisherman Ravik go diving in Rainbow Falls 3 days ago for special seafood, but he never returned”.*  
   
A chill that had nothing to do with the damp air crawled up her spine. If Ravik hadn't returned from the falls, where was the seafood for the feast supposed to come from?. She turned back toward the farmhouse, her boots squelching in the over-saturated earth. Each step felt like the ground was reluctant to let her go, the mud clinging to her soles like tiny, grasping hands. Before she could analyze the situation further, something caught her eye. The farmhouse's mailbox was open wide, a piece of ledger paper dangling out like a lolling tongue. It looked like the inventory sheets Lihara Venn used.

*“The feast went terribly. Seems like the Hexlet jinxed us. How I hate that slug”.*

Iris couldn't deny the pattern. These notes were forming a trail of breadcrumbs leading away from enchantment and toward something much darker. Lihara was known for being meticulous, yet this note was frantic, the ink smeared by what looked like trembling hands.

The air began to feel less like liquid and more like a shroud. She entered the storage shed adjacent to the house, desperate for answers. Inside, the scent of crushed mint was gone, replaced by a sharp, metallic tang. The smell of copper and old pennies seized the room, making her stomach churn. The interior of the shed was a cramped labyrinth of shadows. Iris brushed past heavy iron tools that felt unnaturally cold, their surfaces pitted with a strange, oily corrosion. On a workbench, a set of scales sat tipped to one side, weighed down by a cluster of calcified barnacles. As she pushed aside a stack of moldy fertilizer sacks, a note drifted from a high shelf, fluttering down like a dying bird. It landed next to an empty wooden crate bearing the initials of Tavira Makoa, the water carrier.

*“Tavira found a strange vial labelled ‘Dark Essentia’ that was floating in the lake when she was collecting water today. It looks dangerous”.*

Nearby, Iris noticed scattered blueprints stacked neatly in a corner. They modeled out new farmland, likely in a new cavern. Her instincts took over, alerting her of a potential overlap into the desert. It shook Iris down to the bone, realising the hidden truth. If the jungle was planning to expand past the wall, everything she was tasked to was going to be ruined. The desert couldn't be an expansion. It would be the infection, no, death of the jungle. The final straw came when she found Tavira’s journal, dusty and hidden under a pile of papers. On the very first page, the entry read:

*“I think I'm seeing ghosts. I saw someone who looked like Ravik today, but much greyer. But he's assumed dead, so it can't be him”.*

Iris slammed the book shut as if she had just touched something grotesque. 

The realization hit her harder than a speeding bullet. The shadow of a slug loomed over the shelf behind her right shoulder. She realized, far too late, that the notes weren't just records. They were a warning she had walked right into. As the silhouette grew clearer, Iris recognized the terrifying Vexlet, its very presence seeming to warp the luck of the room.

“Well, well, well…” a voice boomed, echoing through the claustrophobic shed.

The speaker crawled into view, a muscular cave troll, his skin the color of wet slate. He wielded a humongous gatling gun, its chambers loaded not with bullets, but with snarling, twisted Aquafreaks. Crimson veins shot up all around his body, wearing steel plated armor as he grunted loudly.

“What… What are those slugs?” Iris shrieked, terrified. The Aquafreaks gnarled menacingly, their growls breaking her composure.

“They are Aquabeaks… But stronger\!” the troll laughed, pressing the trigger. The Aquafreaks exploded from the barrels, spraying high-pressure jets of water that slammed into the shed's supports. The force was so immense it pushed and separated the ceiling and walls, collapsing the structure and exposing them to the open farmland. Documents, tools, and plans were scrambled into the mud. Everything reeked of wet rot as the darkened Aquabeaks hopped violently back into the gatling gun's chambers.

“You monster\! You're hurting the slugs\! You've turned them into ghouls\!” Iris shouted, trying to defend herself with her Makobreaker. She held the blaster, but her grip was shaky. She was terrified to pull the trigger. The memory of the previous volley, the stinging water and the overwhelming pressure made her hesitate. She didn't want to be injured here, alone, in a place where she was still so much a foreigner.

“Ghouls? That name clicks very nicely… It has a special tingle\!” the cave troll grunted, re-equipping his weapon. “Such a shame that you stand in my way…”.

He raised the weapon, ready to fire again.

“Ravik?” The voice was Salem’s. He had arrived just in time to find Iris on the ground, cornered by the brute. Salem stood at the edge of the clearing, his face a mask of shock and grief. “I thought you were dead…”.
