### The Drunken Desert

Iris gazed upon the drunken desert as it slowly spurred out a wave of scorching sand. Again and again, violent and endless. Her trimmed mahogany hair was already ruined with the sand ensnaring itself between each strand, making her extremely uncomfortable. Even her clothes were all dusted with a layer of the sandy fog, further entrapping Iris in unease. Barely out of sight, huge worm-looking creatures sprawled out, jumping out of the sand as if it was chasing something off into the distance. Nothing but a mirage of overly saturated warm tones enveloped her vision. She didn’t want to believe anything that she saw. Her father had told stories of Slugterra to be a wonderful place. This wasn’t the same place he had described.

It was cruel in the desert. 

Even Iris's dramatic entrance— landing on a small hill of sand was rough, the sand was burning hot from the solaryte crystals above that lit the desert. It felt like she was navigating through a bed of lava. The sand sank deeper with every step she took, making the journey feel evermore exhausting.

“Hello?” 

Silence. 

It felt like nothing could hear her. Nothing. At least, that’s what she thought. Only the gentle trickling of sand carried by the subtle warm breeze sounded, with millions of tiny vibrations surging through the ground. It felt unbalanced, shaky, but it was the nature of the desert. Unpredictable. The heat was already unbearable for her, and Iris didn't know if she could hold on for much longer. 

“Ugh, where do I go? Where'd dad go Fin?” Iris groaned. She was already sweating and turned to Fin, looking at him as if he had the answers. In fact, he did, gliding along happily while Iris was stuck walking through the desert, rubbing the sweat off her forehead every few steps she took. Remnants of machines that seemed to be mechanisms or mecha beasts littered the barren land, signalling to Iris of how dangerous everything was going to be, especially for someone who was tasked to watch over the region. What was even left to watch, if the area was in such a grave condition? It was nothing more than a vast and dystopian area to Iris and couldn’t see why it needed Watchers. What did the Watchers even mean in the Southern Caverns? 

As Iris contemplated her thoughts of her father’s legacy, Sand Anglers peeked from hills, shyly gazing at the lone girl that trotted forward, terrified of who they were. The desert was truly drunken, swirling in disaster and chaos every moment. They needed their Watcher. 

Wilted fauna like cactus stood solitude, almost desaturated adding to the gloominess of the desert. What was there truly left? Eventually, a crooked wooden sign with the word Steamvale etched onto it appeared in the sandy fog. Iris's vision, though slightly blurred from the overheating, trudged tiredly into the small town. Fin, the hoverbug was fine, gliding easily along the desert breeze. Yes, they both wanted water, but at least Fin's spirits were still high. 

The town was a spectacle to say the least as it stood out like a sore thumb in the desert— it wasn’t like the traditional fantasised oasis, but the complete opposite of one. It was like the embodiment of the industrial revolution in a physical form, black smoke rising into the sandy polluted air and strange noises of whirring chimed out and about. It complemented its sere environment, almost as if the crazed mechanisms were trying to fuel and repair the intoxicated surroundings. It was not surprising that the steampunk aesthetic alerted Iris, especially when she saw the lack of people. Was this the capital of the desert? It felt almost abandoned to her. It was too quiet. Pipes wound down all sides of buildings and strange mechanical sounds chirped as Iris continued through the supposed city, though she saw it as more of a town. Was there hope in the barren graveyard? 

“Ugh, I need a drink…I'm out of water already…How could this place even be this hot?” Iris complained. Exhausted, she pushed forwards and walked into a small bar. The interior didn't speak that much apart from the renaissance floors, with even more bizarre contraptions on the counter. Eyes darted around and a few gazed towards Iris as she clumsily stumbled forward.

“Speak of the devil, it's Greggory's kid\!” a voice chimed, surprising Iris. Her father was well known, but the fact she was recognised impressed her. “Eh, what's ‘er name?”

“You've come at a bad time,” another added, resting his blaster on a table. The shaded highlights sparked grim in the dully lit bar. The ambient chatter began stirring Iris's stomach, making her feel uneasy with the amount of attention directed towards her. 

“What's wrong?” Iris exclaimed, her voice racing with curiosity. The chatter swooped speechless, the bar pausing for a beat as Iris's mind raced. She was searching for answers in the eyes of everyone around. Suddenly, the intensity spiked like a fever when her eyes finally narrowed towards the slouched bartender, who seemed disturbed. The bartender, who didn't seem surprised calmy set a glass on the counter.

“He's gone…The wall has taken him…” the bartender mumbled ominously. He leant forward on the counter, and with a disappointed tone, sighed. “You're our new Watcher right? Whatever happens, you make sure nothing comes out of that wall…”

Iris listened with terror, unable to understand how the wall had just consumed her father. She had heard legends of what was beyond the wall, terrifying monsters that were corrupted by something known as dark water. She was terrified, but also drowned in grief with her father's absence. It was his invitation to come down to help him watch the wall. Had he found something?

“What's so important about the wall anyway? Why do I have to watch it?” Iris asked. 

The reluctant bartender stood back up, preparing a drink for Iris as he explained the truth. “You see, we don't know much about why we watch the wall or what's behind it. It's a scary world out there full of monsters, that's all I know,”

Iris waited as if the silence would answer her question, realising that her responsibility was more complex than she thought. What was the purpose of watching?

“You better find him, we don't know anything about watchin’ that towering wall of ours,” one added, fuelling her sharp internal turmoil. Iris chugged the drink the bartender had poured for her, rehydrating her overheated body. Of course, it couldn't cool down the burning worry of her father, but it soothed her enough to listen to the words of those around her. She needed to act, and fast. But Iris knew one thing for certain— she wasn't ready.

Fin flapped worriedly upon Iris's shoulder, wanting to leave the cramped tavern. “C’mon Fin, we need to go\! I need to find a blaster somewhere\!”

As Iris walked out the door and turned back onto the main street, trailing Fin, she found she had wandered down another alley of buildings lined both sides. But this alley felt more broken, more worn down and almost abandoned. One askew hanging sign contrasted with the other murky monotone signs, highlighting in a faint lime glow the words “Gun Shop”. It had the spark to convey that there was still hope and that the store was still alive and the idea that there was something worth saving, so she headed towards the front door. Iris did need a blaster and this was the perfect place to purchase one.

“We're closed\!” the seller yelled grumpily from the counter. Holding a beige duster, Iris watched the man dust the arcane blaster he held, decorated in a slick golden colour. His rugged appearance of a black shirt, tampered with crimson stripes along the shoulders looked frightening, reinforcing his frustrated tone. “Can't ya hear?”

“Hi, I don't know if you know me–” Iris began.

“Would knowing you really change a thing? Women can't sling slugs for floppers\!” he scowled, interrupting Iris. His attitude was quite unusual, almost steering Iris away. 

In an almost inaudible voice, Iris spoke. “Kura. Does that ring a bell?”

“Greggory Kura?” The man paused and looked at Iris for a second. With a scornful and suspicious look, he picked up what seemed to be some type of spectacles, coloured in navy from the desk to inspect her closer. “He'ad a daughter? Argh, that blasted man…”

“Hm? What's wrong with my father?” Iris asked, leaning against the counter. The seller slid the blaster he was holding aside, now intrigued by Iris. Who was she? Did it mean that Greggory had failed his mission? 

“Ain't ya the fellow hoverbug of Greggory's?” the seller exclaimed as he inspected Iris closer. He adjusted the mechanical monocle, zooming in on Fin. Iris found the piece of machinery quite intriguing as well, also spotting a numerous array of other equipment in the store. No, the store wasn’t just a gun store, it was an accessories store filled to the brim with strange contraptions and upgrades to existing technology. Some seemed to be parts specific for mecha-beasts to thrive in desert environments and others were optics for blasters. 

Fin nodded to the seller’s inspection with the seller responding with a simple smirk. “These are dark times, dark times indeed…Since you’re here, it must only mean one thing…”

The man scratched his bald head, pondering deep before he continued his sentence. “I’m assuming you’re going to be the *Watcher*, eh?”

“Yes…? Do you know what I need to do?” Iris asked curiously, hoping that out of everyone, he could answer her question. Her answers hadn’t been direct, it had all been messy and jumbled since she had landed in Slugterra. “Um–”

“Chuck, call me Chuck. Your father's old pal,” Chuck smiled. “It’s fair for ya’ younglings to be clueless, ‘cause aren’t we all?”

Iris related to that statement extremely well, nodding in agreement as she slowly pulled the blaster Chuck had just slid aside. Her eyes were still focused on the conversation, but her mind had raced off, wanting that golden blaster.

“You see, there’s a wall for a reason. It’s to contain the desert. We can’t let anything out, or in the desert. If the wall falls, the Southern Caverns fall. You got that?” Chuck added, explaining the true nature of the Watcher. Iris seemed to understand but by now, the blaster she had groped at stood between the two, with a slight tension rising between them.

“Y’know, that’s one of your father’s old blasters, right? A relic to time. Still works like a charm though,” Chuck laughed, pushing the blaster into her hands. Iris, almost surprised, stumbled slightly and almost dropped the blaster but careful timing saved her. The blaster lightly juggled in her hands until she finally firmly gripped the cold metallic grip, preventing her from accidentally dropping it again.

“For me?” Iris asked, her hands still slightly shaking. It was rather light-weight for a blaster and when Fin jumped into a barrel, Iris couldn’t say no to a test shot. She wanted to feel the satisfaction of shooting a slug. Her first time ever slinging a slug. She aimed her blaster around the store before Chuck finally interrupted her, shouting in his terrifying voice.

“Don't shoot it in here, go outside\!” Iris smiled and ran outside, ready for Fin to rip through the air. As her fingers firmly rested on the trigger, a loud click sounded followed by the accelerator surging up, shooting Fin straight into the sky at a smooth 100km/h. A sharp streak of deep blue carried through the air, lined by a faint zig-zag of white. Fin reached velocimorph, transforming into a much more powerful version of itself, swooping low and performing complex aerobatics. Fin’s loops and swirls in the air were performed gracefully like a ballerina, his movement slick and his accuracy precise. Iris watched in amazement, for it was her first time shooting a slug. 

“Wow\! Thanks, Chuck,” Iris exclaimed, catching Fin as he completed his performance. He jumped up happily in her palm, cheering with his pitched shouts. Iris could see Fin was a bit tired through his paced breath, but knew he still held a high level of spirit and enthusiasm. 

“Oh yeah, your father also wanted you to have my mecha beast. It's basically new, I haven't used her in years,” Chuck informed, gesturing for Iris to follow her. 

“Also, I have another question…What’s beyond the wall?” Again, Iris was laced in curiosity, searching for the true nature of the Southern Caverns. But she didn’t know that not all questions had answers.

“The other side of the wall? No clue. Probably monsters. Real evil monsters…” Chuck mumbled, stroking his chin as he thought about the question. Nobody generally asked questions about the wall as to many people in the desert, the wall was almost like a deity, protecting them from the villainous world. Whatever monsters or creatures that were out there were separated from the desert, and they wanted to be kept that way.  

It wasn’t long before Chuck had led Iris through the city, turning in all sorts of directions to find a small entrance to what seemed to be a truly abandoned section. Among the smaller alleyways and beside rusted steel infrastructure hid a cloaked beast, a mechanical vehicle covered in a white cloth. As Chuck unveiled the Dingo mecha, Iris smiled brightly, astonished at the sight. It wasn't dirty nor rusted, it seemed to be in perfect condition. 

“Woah…” Iris thanked, petting the hood of the beast. She couldn’t resist jumping on the mecha beast, quickly saddling on and feeling the modern comfort of the cushioned seat. 

“It’s a tough world out there, you’re gonna need it,” Chuck explained. He sighed in relief, knowing that with Iris, the desert would remain safe. At least for now, they had a Watcher and in his eyes, someone to continue his friend Greggory’s legacy. 

Butterflies settled afloat inside Iris’s stomach, followed by a chilling sensation that sent goosebumps through her body as she sat quietly on her new mech. She was destined to be the next Watcher. She was needed for the desert’s safety. Gazing upon the town of Steamvale, built from spirals of intricate beams and vintage clockwork, she realised this was the beginning of something new. There was something darker in the outback. “So…What do I do next?”
