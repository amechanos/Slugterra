### Legend of the Drunken Devil

It had been months. Salem didn’t know.

Time flowed strangely in the Temporal Jungle. While he remained, the world beyond the wall had already moved on.

Iris was gone.

The sand sang like a hypnotic tune, the dust dancing in his face. The red eyes softened, fading into a calm mint green. The voice whispered intimately, barely caressing Salem's delicate face as he squinted harder, trying to see through the dust. It felt angelic, yet so ominous as the barren red land had welcomed him— deathly heat that felt like it scorched his heart.

*“No…I'm hallucinating…”* Salem muttered, shaking his head. The dust had slowly dissolved as Salem cleared his mind, trying to ignore the heat temporarily. Then, there it was.

It wasn’t Iris.

Rather, it was a boy, height rather short with a shrub for hair. Salem glanced at him awkwardly as he appeared from the shadows.

“So it's true…” the boy mumbled. A lazy mole appeared by his side, slouched on his wombat for mech. The duo appeared rather peculiar, skewing Salem's first impressions. He didn't know what to expect— these folk did not look like they were from the desert. Their skin wasn't heavily tanned and their aesthetic was overly saturated with modern greens. Darker shades of green barely lingered around, with rather rich earthy tones colouring their mechs. The mole panted like an exhausted dog, as if it was dehydrated. Salem watched the duo with confusion, not able to understand their motive.

“Your slugs…” the boy began as he inspected Salem's figure. Salem's slugs in their slug shells, holstered on to his belt gnarled, emitting a strange red aura that the boy was familiar with. “They're not normal…They've been infected by dark water…”

“What? You know…about this?” Salem asked, his voice pitching up in eagerness. The boy leant closer as the slugs barked ferociously, analysing their nature. 

The boy hesitated. He leant back, correcting his attention back onto Salem who worriedly stared at his Menasher slug. “No, not a lot. But I know for certain that it is dark water— I've only heard stories of their origin…” 

Salem listened patiently, trying to process the new information. His voice tensed, grappling to a second thought. “It can be… reversed, right? I don't want my slugs to be like this…They're…my friends…”

“…Maybe.” the boy mumbled, looking at his molenoid friend. “Nole?”

“Ah yes, the sacred Deep Roots\! Prophesised to be able to cure any disease…” Nole exclaimed proudly, boasting his rather dwarfish charm.

“Do you think it can…heal dark water?” the boy asked.

“Oh, of course it can\! It is the magnificent centre piece of the ridgelands— one drop, and all the impurities of the world are gone\!” Nole elegantly boasted.

Nash sighed, not knowing if he was to trust Nole. Nole's exaggerations were often incorrect and outright inaccurate, never holding up to their potential like how he had described. Nash awkwardly gazed at Nole, then shrugged at Salem signalling his discernment.

“Are you from around here?” Salem asked, sounding rather lost. The heat was already knocking him off balance, his first impressions already down the drain.

“Nope, but I do know where the town is. Just be careful, there's someone named the Drunken Devil terrorising the desert…” Nash smiled, motioning for Salem to follow.

Nole rolled his eyes at Nash's statement, attempting to ease Salem into the desert. He sounded assuring, but again, the exaggeration felt too forced. “Nash, it's just urban legend\! There is no such thing as a Drunken Devil\!”

\~

The ride was mostly silent, only the gnarling of the slugs on Salem’s belt broke it occasionally. Salem would reach down, his fingers brushing the shells that emitted a strange, sickly red aura, trying to pet and calm them down. He could feel their distress. 

The trio  trotted against the barren land with no vegetation in sight for kilometres on end. Just sand and wind. The faint sound of screeching fossil worms echoed, occasionally breaking the eerie silence. Salem closed his eyes, feeling the desert's peace. It was quiet, and surprisingly not as violent as he had thought. 

“We're almost there,” Nash said, finally breaking the silence. A wooden sign, almost worn out, had a barely legible town name of ‘Steamvale’ imprinted on it that Salem took notice of.

“It's so…quiet…I expected so much more…” Salem added, disturbing Nole's peace.

Nash chuckled, almost gagging. “Quiet? Obviously you've never been to the desert. What makes you think this orange hellhole is quiet? It's called the drunken desert for a reason,” 

Salem paused, contemplating his words. Perhaps Nole was right— the stupid mole he saw at first had rather deep, insightful information. It felt too quiet. Something was definitely wrong in the desert. 

Riding into Steamvale was rather surprising to Salem— seeing a steampunk aesthetic that wildly contrasted his previous sights of jungle architecture. It felt so distinct, with corroded metal being the emphasis. The heart of Steamvale hummed with the rumbling of red sand, circling the centre statue of the mythical Midas slug. What caught Salem's eye wasn't the buildings nor diverse culture Steamvale conveyed, rather it was the posters. No, it wasn't propaganda or related to politics, it was a wanted poster of the Drunken Devil. Perhaps Nash was right. It was dangerous. But Salem couldn't shake the details the poster had. He pulled up before the pole that had the poster attached, taking a moment to question the truth. The silhouette looked too familiar with the hair and the star pin stuck into her hair. He lingered around, slowing his pace down. Salem, now curious, approached a poster and snatched it off, analysing it closer. Eyes darted around, many directing their attention to Salem. He slowly gazed at the eyes around him, only to return staring at the poster intensely. Muttering ensued and the chatter only grew as Salem tried to focus. Disturbed, he glanced around as if he had done something wrong. But there wasn't. It wasn't his fault. 

Each building all had the same pinned wanted poster, hanging ominously as Salem moved through town. His mind had wandered off, almost as if his body began to move on its own. It felt like a dream, moving slowly as if he had just entered a brand new world. Otherworldly voices lingered in his head, guiding him to the corners of the city where more and more posters alike with shy families loitered. Parents mysteriously shielded their children as Salem collected more posters, analysing if there was a deeper meaning. Some were drawn on by markers, depicting a more evil version and others had completely crossed out the face as if it was a myth, a legend perhaps. Salem couldn't come to terms with anything. It didn't add up. 

“What's wrong…?” Salem voiced out, hoping for an answer. His question, dubious towards those around him, granted no response. Silence. Salem moved forward, ignoring that behind. He felt uneasy for a moment, something signalling something was wrong. Steamvale felt wrong.

Then, in the corner of his eye he felt a disturbing force ripping through the air. Stripes of blue and orange cried a frightful screech as Salem finally saw the attack; an Armashelt aimed at his face. The violent claw struck before Salem could react, knocking him forcefully off of his mech and skidding him across the sand. Salem pushed himself up, coughing through the dust as his vision blurred. For some bizarre reason, Salem felt and was unharmed even with the Armashelt's direct impact. It was as if the hit felt rather weak, almost as if the slug was trying to hold back. 

The galloping grew louder. A mech burst through the sandstorm. It didn’t slow down. It rammed straight on, knocking over Salem and his mech in its relentless rampage forwards.

“No…It can't be…” Salem winced as he glanced up to see the mech's rider. He laid flat on the ground, the mecha-beast's four legs entrapping his body beneath him. The mechanical vehicle's eyes, tinted burgundy and rims plated in a dark grey roared as the fearless but innocent face of a devilled Iris forcefully gazed down upon Salem's weakened state. Her figure looked larger, firmer and more muscular, as if the dark water had permanently altered her body. Tensed at the translation the poster had depicted was more than a nightmare— it was more ghoulish than he thought. The jagged lines and pale skin the illustration had was nothing compared to seeing Iris in the flesh, right in front of himself.

“Iris…No…You can't be real…What happened?” Salem's voice weakened, whimpering as he tried to piece together what was happening. Her slugs, jittering in fear in their shells, looked distressed, clinging tightly in terror. She could see the change— the effect of what had to be dark water. The very thing that ghouled his own slugs.

“You left me to die…” the devil hissed. Crimson red veins and the pale white skin coursed in Salem's vision, completely lost of where he was. Nash and Nole weren't around. “And this?”

Iris snatched the posters from Salem's hand, looking at the menace she had become. There was nothing left of her. She ripped the posters apart swiftly, the shreds of paper gently floating towards the ground. Her vision was filled with hatred as she stared at Salem deep in the heart. 

“Iris…?” Salem groaned, trying to talk it out. Iris didn't respond. Still cold as ever, but now with demon-like eyes seeking something dangerous. Her blaster swung up, centering in Salem's vision as he tried to scramble back.

“Get em, Fin,” Iris growled, firing her terrified Hoverbug. The Hoverbug emerged, its wings jittering in terror, yet it obeyed the command. Salem felt the slug's wings grasp him tightly, lifting him into the scorching air. He looked down at Iris, hoping for a flicker of the girl he knew, but she only snickered. There was no remorse in her expression, only a disgusting smile as she signaled for Fin to let go. Salem plummeted, the landing brutally painful as he hit the sunburnt sand, the heat leaving fresh burns on his face while Iris sneered rapturously and sped away. Iris sneered rapturously before turning her mech's engine back on.

“Wait…” Salem muttered weakly, reaching for his blaster. But it was too late. Iris was already accelerating fast, speeding out of Steamvale. Salem stood weak, laying on the ground trying to catch his breath. Then, the blue hoverbug appeared. Iris's beloved hoverbug, hopping on his chest. “Fin?”

It chirped quietly, expressing its concern. Salem smiled, petting it gently. Salem was exhausted, with the last pieces of Iris beside her. Fin, her hoverbug clung tight to Salem, trying to comfort him. It was hurt too, scared of Iris. It seemed that hope was lost for getting Iris back. Then, the sound of mechs galloping became audible, growing louder as it approached him. Salem quickly rolled onto his stomach only to see the iconic wombat mech trot along, understanding that Nash had finally found him.

“What happened?” Nash exclaimed as he finally arrived at the scene, seeing Salem in a painful state. 

“Ughhh…” Salem moaned painfully. Still lying on the floor, eyes glanced at the trio barricading the middle of the road. “She's gone…The Drunken Devil…Iris…”

“Who?” Nole asked, looking around trying to find the attacker. Faint stains of blood covered Salem's hands, with his jacket being totaled. It was lucky he hadn't sustained major injuries, finally pulling himself up after a few gruelling minutes. Salem dusted the sand off his shirt, finally standing up.

Salem grunted, then let out a disappointed sigh. “The Drunken Devil…”

“See? I told you she was real\!” Nash scolded, slapping Nole on the head playfully claiming his knowledge as superior.

“What was that for?” Nole snapped, only finding both Nash and Salem to have a good chuckle. 

\~

The three trudged into the nearby tavern, seeking shelter and beverages to cure the heat. The chatter slowly drowned in quick and quiet glances as they took a table near the back.

Some stared.

Some turned away.

A man near the counter clutched his arm, wrapped poorly in cloth stained dark. Another sat slumped over a table, breathing heavily. Sand still clung to their clothes.

Nole was already reaching for a drink like nothing was wrong. It was a drastic change in environment, the interior cooled nicely while the drinks were freshly chilled and had a cool mint flavour. Nole happily sipped on his drink while Nash and Salem began their discussion.

“We have to stop her…” Salem explained. 

Nash sighed, slouching forwards in his chair. “What's up with her anyway? We have no responsibility…”

“No\!” Salem exclaimed, frustrated. “She's my friend…She has a legacy of some sort…I don't know why, but Slugterra needs someone like her…”

Nash exhaled slowly, dragging his hand down his face. “Aren't your slugs *also* your friends?”

Salem froze.

“Yeah, but Iris— She's—”

“I don't know Salem…” Nole continued. “What do you even want to do?”

Suddenly, a loud crash interrupted the conversation, with a heavily worn out man rushing into the tavern. He was out of breath, as if he had just run a marathon.

“Oi—\!” the bartender shouted, rushing forward. “What's wrong?” 

Eyes glanced at the man who panted excessively, unable to form his words.

“She's…in…Clinkton— destroying…town…” Covered in sand and blood, the man collapsed on the spot. 

“I'll go by myself if you don't trust me,” Salem exclaimed, jumping up with determination. “I don't care what you guys think, this is *my* problem and I'm going to fix it.”

“Are you stupid?\! That is suicide\!” Nole yelled. Nash nodded in agreement trying to hold Salem back, but his attitude shook his grip. He easily pushed past the two, barging out of the tavern seeking for hope. The last Nash could see was Salem's careless silhouette disappear into the desert, determined to save his friend.

“No, he doesn't have a plan…” Nash muttered towards Nole, holding him back. “*I* do, though,”
