Inside the Crux
“Woah, so Nash, this is your home?” Salem asked, dazzled at the bizarre view. The group stood at the mouth of a cave that seeped into the true Ridgelands, a true spectacle of rolling hills and fractured landscapes. Towers of canyons and fractured mountains soared overhead from the cavern they stood from, carved low into a deep canyon wall.

In all directions, Iris could only see ravines branching from the canyon, a rather supernatural view to say the least. Greens and earthy tones lined the rocks, while blues spread beneath each archaic layer like blood veins, almost as if the Ridgelands was alive. Iris honestly couldn't believe her eyes. The air was electric, the ground was static and every step she took, it felt like the earth was breathing. 

“Is it supposed to feel like this?” Iris asked, stomping on the ground. It looked and felt natural but there was something off about it. The gravel felt softer, the dirt feeling lighter.

“What do you mean?” Nash questioned, a confused look.

Every step the group took, it felt like the very ground they were moving on was alive. Salem couldn't help but notice the intricate seams that ran along the rock, almost conveying that the canyons could weave and interlock with another.

“No…It's just…what's that word?” Salem began, trying to vividly capture the sensation. “Mystical?”

“Like gods?” Nole asked, nodding to Salem's interpretation.

Nash snapped his fingers, pointing at Salem. “Ah, you must be thinking of Wandjina,”

“Wandjina? What's that?” Salem added, eager of the name’s mystique. On the surface, it sounded like a mystical deity, much like the Rainbow Serpent Salem was familiar with within his home of the jungle. It did align with his description of mystical, so Salem listened curiously.

“Allow me to explain!” Nole interrupted, pushing Nash aside as he took the lead. As the group followed the narrow path that wound round the canyon's wall, Nole began his history lesson. 

“Wandjina is a mystical spirit that lives in the Ridgelands— a deity of all!” Nole began. The group went silent, following eagerly. As Nole motioned and placed emphasis on Wandjina's presence, Salem felt stones falling from overhead, already drawing concern of the pace they were travelling at.

“Well, not moles. We dislike Wandjina— her spirit moves the Earth! Our precious tunnels, homes are often destroyed by the deity!" Nole motioned. As he swung his fist upwards, more stones fell, this time hitting on Nole's head. He gazed up slowly, now with slight panic settling in. “Uhm…Let's move a little quicker, shall we?” 

“Forget it, I'll get Fin to carry us over,” Iris exclaimed, loading her blaster. “Nole, you're going first,”

“What?” Nole yelled. “Why me?” 

Overhead, the cliff budged. A slight disturbance was all it needed, and with Nole's shrill shouts, it was all it needed to nudge the wall to fracture. 

“Less talk!” Iris reacted. Fin circled around before swooping below Nole, raising his mech as he clutched on to his handle bars tightly, afraid of falling into the deep chasm. Though shaky, Fin flew straight for the flat surface, trying to control his flight. As Nole finally landed safely on flat ground, he felt relieved. As he gazed back at the path, the rock had already budged and tumbled down, puncturing the path. Stone debris had flung off, chipping against the canyon as it plummeted into what seemed to be the abyss. The rest of the group watched Nole across the canyon, knowing well that it was their only way out of the situation, their path forward being cut off. It wasn't safe anymore. 

“Shoot Fin back across!” Iris yelled, her voice reaching across the canyon. Fin smirked at Nole's unexpressive frown, launching back across safely into Iris's grasp. It wasn't long until Iris was able to help Nash and Salem across, leaving herself as the final one to cross the canyon. The path only became more unstable by the moment and the worry became clear.

“Hurry up Iris!” Nash called out, slinging her Hoverbug back to her. As Iris caught her Hoverbug, one of her mech's legs collapsed, sinking into the stone. It lurched forwards, its body sliding down as Iris held on to the handle for dear life. 

“Just one more trip, alright Fin?” Iris asked, tossing a chip of slug food towards Fin. “You can do it!” 

Fin, though exhausted, felt motivated by Iris's bright smile and the refuelling of slug food, rehyping his energy by a slimmer. Before Iris could fire Fin again, her mech suddenly lurched forwards, one of its legs sinking down through the stones. Iris grasped onto the handle bars tightly, trying to pull her mech back and swerve it back onto balance. Then, the rumbling began. 

Iris shot her head backwards, watching the cliff wobble violently behind her. She could feel the Earth begin to shake, her mech still unstable, vibrating every second as she tried to pull it back up. It came loose. Before Iris could even react, the rock crushed its force on her mech's hind legs, pushing Iris off her mech, sending her plummeting into what seemed to be the abyss. As her hands slid off her mech, she felt her blaster fall out of its strap, giving her almost no chance to save herself. 

“Fin!!!” Iris yelled, hurling Fin downwards hoping the speed was enough for him to reach velocimorph. “Fin!!!”

Her voice, shrill and sharp, squealed like a terrified girl. It felt impending, almost as if there was almost nothing she could do. She had seen a similar scenario just like this in fiction, and Iris just couldn't warp her mind around it. It felt unreal to be trapped in a situation like this. Nash, Nole and Salem all peeped over, down into the canyon as they watched Iris sink into the darkness. 

“No…” Salem's voice was as terrified as Iris's. He hesitated as he held himself back from jumping down into the canyon himself, realising that this wasn't something he could intervene in. The Ridgelands was foreign to him. Salem’s heart skipped a beat, unable to keep focus. He stepped back. “No…This can't be…”

“No, trust the spirit—” Nash began, closing his eyes.

“Are you joking right now?” Salem clearly distressed. “Aren't you going to do something? She's going to die!” 

“Listen carefully—” Nash whispered, almost as if he was speaking to something of higher descent. “—You can hear the Earth…”

As Fin finally reached terminal velocity, he transformed, finally being able to spread his wings out. His speed significantly reduced, now hovering for a moment before continuing to dive down. Nole was still fixed on Fin diving towards Iris, like a dog chasing its tail. It was cruel. But instead of the terrified emotion Salem was showing, both Nole and Nash tilted their heads, waiting for it to happen. Iris' voice had already faded, her breath croaking in despair. Her eyes felt the heavy pressure of gravity sealing them, gently accepting the force that had pulled her down. The weeping silence of the Western winds violently gushed onto her body, propelling her towards the mysterious black cloud that sat dormant at the bottom of the canyon. Still, flowing along gravity and still accelerating just couldn't reach Iris in time as the black canyon fog below obscured Fin's vision. But with love on the line, Fin dove straight into the darkness, dispelling the mist that shrouded the canyon's end for a brief moment before it engulfed the shallow opening back. Both their figures had disappeared and now the rest of the group had lost their track on Iris and Fin.

Then, there was nothing. 

For a moment, Salem dropped his head low, paying his respects before a gentle poke from the depths of the canyon echoed to the surface. A sliver of sound, cracking from the darkness had resurrected hope as Salem peered down to see the commotion.

The fog had dispersed, revealing the hellish depth of the canyon where Iris and Fin were falling. Then, like a wolf in sheep's fur was the strangely protruded piece of rock that stood like a massive, horizontal spire. The dark, basaltic rock that suddenly appeared, perhaps extruded just then by what Nole had described to be Wandjina. It was like an iron javelin that stabbed into the cliff face, finally diffusing the black fog.


Then, there was an aquamarine streak glided across the canyon. Iris and Fin, like speckles of dust still free falling suddenly disappeared from view, unknown of what had happened.

“Where’d…they go?” Salem stuttered, confused. It felt unreal, having only healed Iris back to her normal form moments ago and now she had just disappeared. It felt to Salem it was all for nothing. As Salem began his next sentence, a blinding flash hurled Iris on to the ground beside Salem, all safe and sound. “Iris! What? How is this possible?”

“Wandjina—” Nash began. “That’s what Wandjina is. I guess it’s better if you see it in action. You saw that canyon wall right?”

Salem paused, trying to remember the sequence of actions. He peered back down into the canyon, the protruded ledge-like piece of rock still there. It felt so off, just sticking out like a sore thumb. It didn’t blend into the Earth. “Wandjina…”

“I guess Nash hasn’t explained everything! Iris appeared here because of the Chasm slug! It is a teleportation slug!” Nole smiled, trying to cheer Salem up. Nash helped Iris up, regaining her balance as she tried to understand what happened.

“Was that…Wandjina?” Iris asked, now fully aware of her surroundings. She wasn’t falling, but her mind still felt like it was falling. To her, it was a miracle to still be alive. She stepped forward and gazed down into the canyon again, trying to see what had happened. “So…so bizarre…”

“No, apparently a slug saved you…” Salem explained. Salem reached out, grasping Iris tightly before giving her a warm hug. “I thought I had lost you again…” he weakly muttered.

Nash sighed, moving towards his mech. “Look, I’ll get you a new blaster and mech. We’ve got some distance to go if we want to get to Askent Town…”

“I need to get my mind off of this…” Salem gathered himself together, hopping back on his mech. “Iris, you wanna hop on?”

Before long, Askent Town was ahead. The road narrowed as the group crested the final rise, and the town revealed itself all at once.

For a long moment, none of them spoke.

Right ahead of them rose a colossal pillar of stone, broad enough to hold an entire settlement upon its crown. The rock thrust from the earth like the spine of some ancient buried giant, its sides sheer and weathered by centuries of wind. Around its base yawned a deep canyon that circled the town like a moat carved by the gods themselves. Thin streams of mist drifted through the chasm below, pale against the darkness of the depths. Somewhere far beneath, water echoed faintly against stone.

“Holy flopper…” whispered Salem.

Fragile wooden drawbridges stretched across the canyon, connecting what seemed to be a town skewed on a side to the vast mainland of the ridgelands. The air changed as they approached. Out on the road, the wind had smelled of dry grass and wet dirt. Contrasting the organic tone, the town reeked of coal smoke and a distinct scent of damp, basaltic stone, sharp enough to sting the nose. 

“This…is Askent town?” Iris asked, still stunned. 

Nash laughed. “Wait until you see Bassecreuse!”

Everything felt mystical. The whole town was askew, leaning upwards the deeper the town went. Iris brushed her hand along the detailed granite walls, feeling each brick protrude out like heavy armour. It felt less like a fortification meant for war and more like a collection of sturdy, overlapping shells built to withstand the elements. The smooth stones and heavy bricks were warm to the touch, retaining the fading heat of the solaryte crystals despite the damp chill rising from the canyon depths. Amber lanterns, slicked with oil that gave off a warm, golden glow, hung from low wooden fences and overhanging eaves. They cast long, dancing shadows across the uneven cobblestones. 

“Watch your step here,” Nash called back, his boots knocking rhythmically against the wooden planks. ‘

The tilt of the town became immediately apparent the moment they stepped past the threshold of the bridge. The ground sloped upward toward the south, forcing them to lean into the climb. It was a bizarre, disorienting sensation. Small, terraced plots of vibrant green vegetables and climbing ivy clung to the sides of the stone dwellings, softening the harsh, earthy tones of the architecture, complementing the tilt.

Salem tilted his head, trying to align his vision with a chimney that was puffing out thick, dark coal smoke. “It’s like the whole place is askew...”

“That’s Askent Town for you,” Nash smiled. “Inn’s just up here.” 

Nash gestured toward a long, low-slung building where the opal light spilled generously onto the path. The sign above the door creaked gently in the canyon breeze, depicting a stylized stone pillar under a painted crescent moon. With a collective chuckle, the group followed Nash toward the warm invitation of the doorway, leaving the misty depths of the canyon and the tilting streets of Askent Town.
