### The Dreaming, Part 1

Iris's eyes fluttered closed. The darkness lingered into her body. Nash couldn’t believe his eyes as he watched Iris fall to the ground. His sense of hope weakened, kneeling beside her as he tried vigorously to try to wake her up.

As the darkness finally captured her, the scene was formed— an aura of a sea blue surrounded her vision before she felt her body being thrown to the ground, collapsing as she fell face down into the soft, tranquil waters of the magical landscape. Iris shielded her gaze from the heavenly light as she looked up, then with a sudden burst of light, two majestic slugs formed from the ashes of nothing appeared. Before she knew it, she found herself kneeling before two god-like slugs. 

*“What has humanity become?”* She thought for a moment. 

“Where…where am I?” Iris weakly groaned as she sat up in the dream-like dimension. It was like that time again, the random occurrences where her mind had tapped into a different subconsciousness.

*We are in danger. Ttolamahwe.* 

“What?” The language, mystic by nature, illogical by law. Iris casted her hand out, her fingers piercing straight through the figures of the slugs. The more she put her hand through the figure formed by the strange mist, a strengthening aura gave in, almost as if energy had materialised itself and formed these slugs. 

Iris felt the energy flow out of her palm like water, the intricate strands of energy dissolving at her sight and reforming the shapes her hand had phased through. Thoughts were sealed in her mind, unable, wonderstruck to even speak out a single word. Her lips twitched, wanting to say something, as if she wanted to say “Who are you?”

*Remember us, child. We are Yhi and Bahloo. Warden what you must.*

The beating shouts of Nash echoed above. She could hear him scream her name, the frightened, cowardish side of him showing. The words felt heavy, carrying a challenging burden as Iris fell frozen, her hands stopping in motion while the energetic mist quickly dissipated.

“Hang on Nash…” Iris whispered, expecting her voice to reach him. The gentle shouts of Nash echoed down like tidal waves, sending vibrations throughout the dome enclosed space. The dome rippled in effect, ridges curving and bending as it slowly faded in strength as the screams stopped. The ambience of the teal glow stood, and Iris sat in the middle, listening to the strange spirits.

*What good does it do? For you are in the mindscape, the intersection of human and slug energy. What you draw becomes that, for senses become unity.*

The tense heat rose, Iris feeling uneasy as the slug spirits whispered the cryptic words. Suddenly, her body jerked back, her head twisted backwards and neck snapped backwards. Facing directly upwards, Iris's body floated, the mindscape collapsing as Iris finally opened her dilated crimson eyes. Blue liquid, reminiscent of the environment around began to violently leak out of her pupils, as if she was suddenly possessed by a spirit. Her body bent, folded like a piece of paper before her figure in the real world began to float a little too. Nash jumped back, terrified as if Iris had been infected by the dark water again. But she wasn't. She was blind— only darkness. The pressure grew, concentrating towards her chest. And then, there was nothing. 

Silence skipped a beat, creeping up on Nash as he kept his distance.

Within the dreamscape, Iris felt the energy pulling her body apart. But as it channelled deeper, something in the real world felt different. A sensation of like a bullet, racing towards her physical body. The presence of a singular, unknown entity haunted her. Being restrained in the mindscape, she shouted, but her physical body didn't react. She couldn't warn Nash.

“Iris\! Iris\! Please wake up\!” Nash cried from afar. Still afraid to draw distance, Nash couldn't bear to see her suffer, forcing himself to confront his new fear. Inching away, the sound of something cut through the air. It was a slug. “Iris\!”

Nash's hand gripped onto her body, finally holding her and feeling the strangely warm skin of her forearms. It was unusual, a strange sense that also surged into Nash's body. He felt the slug creepering closer. “Iris\! We're gonna—”

Before Nash could finish his sentence, the impossible cavern moved, the rocks shifting to create a passage just large enough for a slug. The soaring shriek of the Chasm flooded in, its cyan sparks removing them from the ancient lab. For a moment, Nash felt his soul becoming unchained to his body, an ethereal moment where he felt the raw, ruthless power of the slug energy that enchanted the land.

As he watched the chaos separate his body, he felt himself being transferred far away, the slug's teleportation mechanism finally kicking in. The next moment, Nash was on his mech back on the surface, with Iris slumped forwards in her mech. It felt over. Her body was at least not floating anymore. Nash approached her, helping her up as Iris’s body finally twitched, her eyes weakly opening. “Iris\! Are you alright?” 

A tear ran down her cheek before words formed. No, not even words, rather mutters that were barely legible. “Something…about gods…”

Her voice, more shaken and scared than understandable, only reinforced Nash’s beliefs. This was wrong. Nole and Shrue had to be stopped. Nash almost collapsed, but held his spirit with resilience as he fought on the internal pain. “Dark…Dark water. That's Shrue's plan. They don't know. We need to—”

“No…” Iris weakly groaned. As she used her last bit of strength to finally sit upright in her mech, she was able to piece together a coherent sentence. “No…We need to get that capsule…”

As Iris and Nash traversed the Ridgelands towards the Deep Roots, Nash led, guiding Iris through a new area that he knew was a shortcut.

“Welcome to Alpine Peaks…” Nash smiled, his mech climbing the steep trek of the mountainous region. The slope down was steep, but from the height, the two were able to see the faint glow of the Deep Root tree in the distance. They were not far. 

Hoverbugs and Tormatos hovered nearby, gliding from ledge to ledge as the two slowly hiked down the mountain path. Iris listened as Nash told stories. As the trees lined the untouched land, there was serenity. The cool breezes casted from the heights of the Alpine Peaks brushed along Iris's face, finally letting out a much needed long breath.

“Has Wandjina ever been like this?” Iris began, closing her eyes to embrace the wilderness of the Ridgelands. “For a god who annoys Ridgelanders, how come Wandjina has not moved?”

Nash sighed heavily. The truth felt unbearable to say, but it had to be told. “Well, what Nole has told you is wrong. One thing you might notice between us and moles, it's that we dislike them. There's a reason Verdud exists,” Nash explained. “The whole Wandjina is a god is a hoax…It's more like slug energy…”

“Really?” Iris asked. “What do other RIdgelanders think?”

"It’s common knowledge. I thought moles were smarter than us. The moles think they're fighting a god," Nash said quietly. "But what if Wandjina is the only thing stopping the Ridgelands from becoming something worse?" 

As they crossed the ravine that split Alpine Peak, just out of view, the silhouette of a cave troll appeared.

“Isn't that one of Nole's subordinates?” Iris whispered. Nash took out his rifle and used his scope to confirm the identity. It couldn’t be missed— the bulky shape, it was certain that it was a cave troll. 

Nash tensed, quickly lowering his rifle. “This doesn’t make sense…That shape…Is he holding the capsule?”

“Doesn’t matter— We’re going after him\!” Iris exclaimed, wasting no time to start accelerating towards the pacing cave troll. Nash followed closely, right behind Iris as they began to pick up speed, trailing behind the cave troll.

Iris grappled onto her blasters. Dual-wielding her newly dubbed pistol blasters, she slung a Taranticle in one while her other blaster held her Armashelt. 

“Go\! What are you waiting for?\!” Nash yelled as the two chased the cave troll. Iris pressed her fingers down on the soft trigger. The slugs exploded out of the blaster, the accelerometer blinking with a quick bolt of red. 

The Armashelt crashed into the ground, using its claws to shoot up a section of ground. The cave troll dodged, the container still sealed. 

“C'mon\!” Iris grunted, gripping her accelerate handle harder. Her Taranticle shot forwards, but fell short of the mech. Nash's speedstinger followed through as backup, finally in the fight. The cave troll grunted before turning around and firing a Bubbaleone, deflecting Nash's shot.

“Flopper\!” Nash exclaimed, dodging his own shot. The cave troll reacted instinctively, firing a Polero at Nash's mech. The red and blue rope of its body quickly tangled his mech's legs, causing Nash to be flung forwards.

“Nash\!” Iris cried, almost pressing her breaks.

“Don't worry about me\! Get that capsule\!” Nash yelled. Something inside Iris had ignited again, a pain of immeasurable force burning again. Then, it struck her. Her body froze for a moment, then glitched like a static screen. A flow of energy swept through her fingertips before the ground bellowed. The ground beneath the cave troll suddenly ruptured. Debris and stone exploded upward. Iris threw her arm across her face as dust and pebbles filled the air. When it settled, a jagged spire stood where the mech had been. The deathly spike tore through the ground, ripping the cave troll's mech in half instantly. The cave troll, injured by the great fall, laid on the rocky ground, paralysed.

“What in God's plan…” the cave troll moaned. The pain swelled in his legs, disfigured in a grotesque scene. Iris skidded to a stop as she approached the defeated cave troll. “This coincidence…Is Wandjina even conquerable?”

In the distance, Nash emerged from the hill's incline, finally nearing Iris. What stood in front of him— the spire and the fallen cave troll displayed as a murder scene to him, his mind racing with questions.

“Nash…” Iris began. “The capsule…It's not here…”

When Iris unsheathed the cloth to the box, all there was was a rack of slugs stored horizontally. The slugs whimpered, confused and scared. The cave troll glanced up again, before staring straight into Iris’s soul.

“These slugs…You wretched humans…No wonder the moles hate you all…You hurt the innocent…” the cave troll mumbled. But the slugs in the rack thought otherwise, something spiritual awakening. Both Nash and Iris could feel the strange sensation, almost as if the slug energy within the slugs echoed messages to them, connecting them to the land. Nash glanced east, the silhouette of the domes of Verdud slowly fading into the view as the frightening dust of the attack finally settled.

“Nole must have everything prepared…” Nash realised he was too slow. “Iris, use the Chasm slug to get to Nole. If anyone can convince him, it's you. I'll help you with your mech.”

“No Nash. I thought you were friends with Nole— Come, we need to go together…” Iris's voice trembled, terrified at what she had done. “C'mon, we need to hurry…”
