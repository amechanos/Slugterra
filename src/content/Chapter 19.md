### The Dreaming, Part 1

Iris's eyes fluttered closed. The darkness lingered into her body, then with a sudden burst of light, two majestic slugs formed from the ashes of nothing appeared. Iris, shielding her gaze from the heavenly light crawled up, crossing her legs back as she approached the figures.

“Where…where am I?” Iris weakly groaned as she sat up in the dream-like dimension.

*We are in danger. Ttolamahwe.* 

“What?” The language, mystic by nature, illogical by law. Iris casted her hand out, her fingers piercing straight through the figures of the slugs. The more she put her hand through the figure formed by the strange mist, a strengthening aura gave in, almost as if energy had materialised itself and formed these slugs.

*Remember us, child. We are Yhi and Bahloo. Warden what you must.*

The beating shouts of Nash echoed above. She could hear him scream her name, the frightened, cowardish side of him showing. The words felt heavy, carrying a challenging burden as Iris fell frozen, her hands stopping in motion while the energetic mist quickly dissipated.

“Hang on Nash…” Iris whispered, expecting her voice to reach him.

*What good does it do? For you are in the mindscape, the intersection of human and slug energy. What you draw becomes that, for senses become unity.*

The tense heat rose, Iris feeling uneasy as the slug spirits whispered the cryptic words. Suddenly, her body jerked back, her head twisted backwards and neck snapped backwards. Facing directly upwards, Iris's body floated, the mindscape collapsing as Iris finally opened her dilated crimson eyes.

Nash jumped back, terrified as if Iris had been infected by the dark water again. But she wasn't. She was blind— only darkness. All she could feel was the presence of a singular entity: The Chasm slug. It was speeding towards them, prepared to grant them the epic escape.

“Iris\! Iris\! Please wake up\!” Nash cried, kneeling beside her. Her body twitched, her sight returning. A tear ran down her cheek before she finally saw the cave's ceiling, the hopeful calm light that restored Nash's faith. “Iris\! Are you alright?” 

“What happened? I saw gods…Something is wrong…” Iris muttered, her voice terribly shaken and scared. 

Nash almost collapsed, but held his spirit with resilience as he fought on the internal pain. “Dark…Dark water. That's Shrue's plan. They don't know. We need to—”

Before Nash could finish his sentence, the impossible cavern moved, the rocks shifting to create a passage just large enough for a slug. The soaring shriek of the Chasm flooded in, its cyan sparks removing them from the ancient lab. For a moment, Nash felt his soul becoming unchained to his body, an ethereal moment where he felt the raw, ruthless power of the slug energy that enchanted the land.

The next moment, Nash was on his mech back on the surface, with Iris slumped forwards in her mech. She quickly awoke from the sudden transportation, noticing the Chasm slug joining their party.

“Iris, we need to go now if we want to stop them. The Chasm must want us to bring our mechs, plus I don't think it'll be able to teleport us that far. You were out for a long time,” Nash explained as he helped Iris regain her balance.

Iris smiled warmly, appreciative of Nash's support. “Nash, I've wanted to ask…What are the legends? Who are Yhi and Bahloo?”

As Iris and Nash traversed the Ridgelands towards the Deep Roots, Nash narrated like a poet of the legends of the Ridgelands, immersing Iris in the mystic side of things. 

“Well, what Nole has told you is wrong. One thing you might notice between us and moles, it's that we dislike them. There's a reason Verdud exists,” Nash explained. “The whole Wandjina is a god is a hoax…It's more like slug energy…”

“Really?” Iris asked. “What do other RIdgelanders think?”

"It’s common knowledge. I thought moles were smarter than us. The moles think they're fighting a god," Nash said quietly. "But what if Wandjina is the only thing stopping the Ridgelands from becoming something worse?" 

As they crossed the ravine, just out of view, the silhouette of a cave troll appeared.

“Isn't that one of Nole's subordinates?” Iris whispered. Nole took out his rifle and used his scope to confirm the identity.

Iris grappled onto her blasters. Dual-wielding her newly dubbed pistol blasters, she slung a Taranticle in one while her other blaster held her Armashelt. 

“Go\! What are you waiting for?\!” Nash yelled as the two chased the cave troll. Iris pressed her fingers down on the soft trigger. The slugs exploded out of the blaster, the accelerometer blinking with a quick bolt of red. 

The Armashelt crashed into the ground, using its claws to shoot up a section of ground. The cave troll dodged, the container still sealed. 

“C'mon\!” Iris grunted, gripping her accelerate handle harder. Her Taranticle shot forwards, but fell short of the mech. Nash's speedstinger followed through as backup, finally in the fight. The cave troll grunted before turning around and firing a Bubbaleone, deflecting Nash's shot.

“Flopper\!” Nash exclaimed, dodging his own shot. The cave troll reacted instinctively, firing a Polero at Nash's mech. The red and blue rope of its body quickly tangled his mech's legs, causing Nash to be flung forwards.

“Nash\!” Iris cried, almost pressing her breaks.

“Don't worry about me\! Get that capsule\!” Nash yelled. Something inside Iris had ignited again, a pain of immeasurable force burning again. Then, it struck her. Her body froze for a moment, then glitched like a static screen. A flow of energy swept through her fingertips before the ground bellowed. The ground beneath the cave troll suddenly ruptured. Debris and stone exploded upward. Iris threw her arm across her face as dust and pebbles filled the air. When it settled, a jagged spire stood where the mech had been. The deathly spike tore through the ground, ripping the cave troll's mech in half instantly. The cave troll, injured by the great fall, laid on the rocky ground, paralysed.

“What in God's plan…” the cave troll moaned. The pain swelled in his legs, disfigured in a grotesque scene. Iris skidded to a stop as she approached the defeated cave troll. “This coincidence…Is Wandjina even conquerable?”

In the distance, Nash emerged from the hill's incline, finally nearing Iris. What stood in front of him— the spire and the fallen cave troll displayed as a murder scene to him, his mind racing with questions.

“Nash…” Iris began. “The capsule…It's not here…”

Nash glanced east, the silhouette of the domes of Verdud slowly fading into the view as the frightening dust of the attack finally settled.

“Nole must have everything prepared…” Nash realised he was too slow. “Iris, use the Chasm slug to get to Nole. If anyone can convince him, it's you. I'll help you with your mech.”

“No Nash. I thought you were friends with Nole— Come, we need to go together…” Iris's voice trembled, terrified at what she had done. “We need to fix what's *broken*…not the world.”

“I presumed you'd finally arrive, Nash,” Nole smirked. “What have you two been up to?”

“None of your business. This needs to end, *now*.” Nash exclaimed. 

The emphasis disturbed Nole, his tone snapping back. “You and fighting Wandjina— Are you going to do this or not? Was your expedition to the desert all for nothing?”

“No\! I got Iris…and Salem…” 

“And? You’re just using them\! You promised me you would save the Ridgelands\!”

Iris gasped, unsure what even was unfolding.

“No…Iris…Don’t get it wrong…” Nash’s tone declined, Iris almost feeling bad.

“You humans are wrong\! While you live your lives, us moles are trying to restore our lives\!” Nole shouted, clearly frustrated at Nash. His voice hissed like a dirty viper.  “Dirty traitor…”

“But…isn’t that what makes us human? What’s your next challenge once you overcome Wandjina? What do you live for?” Salem pondered. 

“Don’t throw philosophical remedies at me, I’m done here. I’m going back to Verdud,” Nole announced, storming out of the conversation. Nash had never seen Nole this tempered, but he knew it was a topic that deeply resented with him. But either way, Salem, Nash and Iris couldn’t let Shrue complete his plan. Dark Water was evil enough, and cursing the Ridgelands would have devastating effects, more than just destroying Wandjina completely.

“Nole\!” Nash shouted. “Wandjina isn’t the problem…It’s us…” 

Nole's expression faded, an impulsive demonic one taking over. He felt insulted. 

“Wandjina isn't what we think it is\! Our distinction— Mole and Human— We're so apart…So far apart that–”

Before Nash could finish his sentence, out of pure anger and fury, Nole couldn't resist his loyalty blinding. Nole's Thresher cannonballed towards him, its blades ripping into his arm as his body launched into the rocky walls. He cried out in pain, where Wandjina reacted with every struggle. Finally, the Thresher returned to protoform, leaving a deep wound in Nash's dominant right arm.

“Apart? You have done nothing for the better\!” Nole spat, walking away like nothing had happened. Iris rushed towards Nash, covering the wound with a cloth. Salem, disgraced, aided Nash together, helping him up as the three knew that something terrible was brewing. They needed to act fast.
