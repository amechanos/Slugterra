### Healing What We Lost
The air felt cool, the scorch of the desert was no more. The stem flickered a dull green, faint, but visible. The stone below the team was moist to the touch, nothing like the desert. Tumblestone Cavern was no more. 

“Thank the heavens!” Nole exclaimed, breaking the silence. It was still dark around, Nole reaching for his Flaringo for light. “The Chasm saved us!”

“The…what?” Salem grumbled, still disorientated. Iris still laid asleep, motionless on the floor. Salem shot a quick glance at his surroundings, completely blinded by Nole's Flaringo. “Where are we?”

Nash appeared, seeming rather awake than the rest. “The Deeproots. It's the Chasm's doing. Chasm is a teleportation slug— it's strong,”

Salem gently raised Iris's body back onto her mech, carefully leaning her against her mech's dashboard. “Looks like she's gonna wake up soon…how do we activate the healing?”

“You don't— it just happens!” Nole exclaimed as he pointed to Salem's slugs. Particles of a gentle neon green floated like beads of rain, slowly transcending down upon his slugs. It soothed Salem as he felt the fragile droplets warmly grazing his skin, breaking the dark water's curse. Similarly, Iris's body began to mystically float inches above her mech, a force gently lifting her as the particles emitted from the roots saturated her skin, removing the bleak, pale skin the dark water had given her. Her crimson red veins faded, now to a much simple colour and her body twitched— she had finally woken up.

“Where…am I?” Iris whimpered, waking up weakly, sitting on her mech. Fin who still accompanied Salem excitedly jumped onto Iris, happy to see her back to her normal form. Salem smiled hazily as Nash grinned cheerfully aside. “Who are you two…?”

“Iris…You've missed so much…” Salem began, almost at a loss of words. The Deeproots had worked perfectly, quickly healing both Iris and Salem's slugs. “We're not in the desert anymore…”

“We're in a region called the Ridgelands, just West of the desert,” Nash explained, holding out his hand. “Nash Wallace, and this mole here is my good pal Nole Strewth!” 

Iris warmly shook their hands, swaying her hair back as she reorganised herself. There was still a slight lingering unease within her, almost as if the dark water wasn't fully healed. Perhaps it took time, she thought, ignoring the slight disturbance it began to cause for her. “Uh…So where do we go now?”

“Follow me, I think I know a way out of here,” Nash motioned, already trotting forwards on his mech through the cramped cave.

Is this the girl we seek?

The voice rang in Iris's head, causing her to press her breaks firmly. “Did you guys hear that?” 

“Hear what?” Nole asked, looking back at Iris who had stopped dead in her tracks.

“I didn't hear anything,” Salem assured, continuing forwards right behind Nash. 

Hear us, but not others. We are Yhi and Bahloo, spirits in your dreams. We need not your help, but see your potential. Is it a state you shall seek?

Iris remained still, trying to articulate the voices present in her head. It felt so otherworldly, as if the Deeproots were directly communicating with her. She could feel something strange radiating off of the roots, something more than just particles of healing. It felt celestial, something straight out of a fantasy.

“Hello? Who are you? Why do you want me?” Iris said, facing the roots as if it were a person. Nash glanced back, curious. Salem and Nash slowed their pace, awaiting for Iris to catch up.

Dear child, whatever you seek, you shall find. Whatever you dream, thoughts will bind. When the darkness comes, your dreams can blind!

The echoed reverberations of the voice lingered in her head, Iris unable to shake the feeling away.

“C'mon Iris!” Salem shouted. Iris shook her head fuzzily, quickly catching up. It felt so bizarre. It felt like spiritual gods had invaded her mind, her dreams, setting themselves deep into her body. As the team slowly moved forwards, spider webs seemed to form around them, masking the unearthly cavern ahead.

“Nash, are you sure this is the right way?” Nole said, pointing towards the various spiders crawling along the walls. Arachnets and Taranticle slugs littered the view, playfully spitting webs and posing for silly games. Salem watched in awe as he inspected every corner, finding archaic objects lost to time stuck on cobwebs in the corners of the cavern. 

“We have to go through here, right?” Nash asked, almost as if he was uncertain. “Funnelweb Cavern?”

Nole stared at Nash, hesitation in his eyes. “Are you serious?” 

Nonetheless, Nole could only follow Nash as he couldn't recall a different path out, additionally the terrain had shifted somehow. Nole remembered what had happened just recently, the tunnel connecting the Ridgelands and the Desert through Tumblestone Cavern had disappeared. Something must have happened. 

Funnelweb Cavern wasn't an ordinary cavern; it winded and bended, like a maze that mirrored the broken emotions the group had gone through. It was messy to the extent every move there were cobwebs, masking hidden intentions and covering up emotions. Diggrix and Spikeshades peeped out, overhearing the group’s conversations, quietly snickering behind the shadows. It was clearer than ever that the cavern’s notoriety was beyond the standard, with the arachnids further fuelling the grim and fearful tone of the environment. “Don’t touch those holes. Spikeshades might pop out— their spikes are deadly if touched,”

Salem peeped down the bizarre holes that lined the cavern walls, only to be surprised by a purple slug that popped out. Like a porcupine, the slug's body was covered in the deadly spikes Nash had warned about. 

“Great…” Salem mumbled, trying not to think of them. His mind was still flooded by the environment's mysticism, where the sound of creepingly loud clicks against the walls began pounding through his ears. No, there weren't just slugs here, there was something larger.

“Damn, there are still Arachnids here…” Nash said, his voice lowering as if a monster was lurking beneath the shadows. Then, a scurry of footsteps trotted behind the group, quickly shadowing itself back into the darkness. More clicking pounded rhythmically, as if a long clickety leg tapped against the solid stone like a door, waiting to ambush the group. 

“What was that?” Salem exclaimed, alerted by the strange noise.

“Arachnids, just larger versions of spiders with venomous stingers— Move slow here…” Nash explained, slowing down. As he firmly squeezed the brakes, now coming to a halt, the sound of the arachnid became clearer. It was as clearer than before, the skittering of its body moving swiftly along the surfaces. 

“Above…!” Nash exclaimed, pointing towards the ceiling. Nole frantically put his Flaringo out for no reason, dimming all the light there was as the darkness shrouded the group, making the attack invisible. “Lights! Nole!” 

Panic settled into Nole as he cast his Flaringo back out. But there was nothing. As the small flame trickled around the corners, a singular, lone Taranticle stood atop a small mushroom, waiting. It looked innocent— rather too innocent as the group tried searching for an explanation. 

Then, it pounced. Wedged between a gap within the ceiling, the Arachnid pounced down, leaping onto Nash. Its legs wrapped around his body, grappling him tight as the white fluid began to squirt out and form a web that cocooned him.

“No!” Iris yelled, swiping the Taranticle and loading it. Her finger reacted instantly, pressing down on the trigger hard as the slug launched out, preparing its attack. The slug's tentacle-like legs rushed out, piercing the giant arachnid right through its skull with the tip of its powerful legs, instantly killing it. A loud splatter of a blue fluid oozed out, forming a pool at the cavern's floor. Salem watched in shock as the Taranticle had easily defeated the beast.

“Nash! Are you okay?” Nole exclaimed, grabbing his Thresher slug to cut the web that had already somehow entangled his legs. 

“Yeah, I'm fine. Just surprised, honestly,” Nash smiled, wiping the gooey blood off his face. The Taranticle chirped happily as Nash finally got up, moving away from the beast's blood. It was already messy enough, for the group still had to get out of the cavern. 

“Hey little guy, aren't you a strong one?” Iris smiled awkwardly, petting the Taranticle. Still, some members had different ideas. The escape had already faded to a second priority, with Salem and Iris fully immersed in the glamorous Ridgelands. It was something else. The maroon slug grinned, hopping on her hand and rubbed its head against her arm. “Awe…”

“Taranticle slugs…” Salem muttered, watching Iris befriend it. “Wow…”

The taranticle slug playfully jumped off of Iris and skittered towards the blue liquid, happily licking it. Other Arachnets and Taranticles joined in, feasting on the blood. The group watched in disgust, Nole surprisingly reaching in to taste it. 

“Nole…Don't…” Nash began, signalling for him to move away. But Nole couldn't resist, and with a swipe of a finger, he scooped up a fingertip full of the blue fluid and tasted it. It was a crude bitter taste, with a strange overly sweet after taste that Nole instantly spat out, almost gagging. Salem and Iris could only face palm, with no words to say. “Can we get out of this spiderinfested cavern?”

“Sure,” Iris smiled, sliding her new Taranticle friend into a shell. “I've explored enough, and I've definitely seen enough spiders…”

“Great! Let's go!” Nole smiled sarcastically, swallowing the lingering taste that clung to his tongue. As the group finally approached the long awaited exit, webs barricaded the exit. Nole, still dramatically confident, loaded his trusty Thresher into his blaster. With a firm click, the Thresher launched out, curling up to aim its bladed spin against the path ahead, gliding gracefully forwards and slicing the webs as easily as paper. “Aha! Finally, out of this wretched cavern!”
