### The Ridgeland's Secret, Part 2

When the sound of mechs crossed the stretch of flat land, the terrain shattered like glass, forming uniform and geometric tiles. Before the shining of light cast by the Solaryte crystals, the four figures glided across the steep incline. Diggrix and Dirt Urchins sat behind rocks, silhouettes hopping up and down. To Nole, it felt close.

“How much longer are we going to have to travel?” Iris whined, her voice filled with frustration and exhaustion. They had been travelling for hours on end and the Solaryte crystal's heat only warmed up. 

“According to Nole, it's close. Just hold on Iris,” Nash smiled, handing Iris a small flask of water. As the group reached the top of the hill, a fractured landscape awaited in front of them. Deep, cataclysmic cracks sprawled outwards from the ground, mimicking broken glass— a pure tapestry of chaos. 

Salem paused for a moment, trying to understand where the so-called Midnight Cavern was. “This is it…?”

“Yep…” Nole grinned, casually napping in his mech. “The abandoned labs of the legendary molenoid Surch…”

“Cut the stories. Everyone knows that Midnight Cavern is a legend. How'd you even find this place?” Nash rolled his eyes.

“Wasn't me. Shrue found it\!” Nole smiled, having almost no care in the world as he began to nap away on his mech.

\~

When the energy faded from the Solaryte crystals, one crack finally opened up. Like an unstable rift, a deafening tremor ripped the earth apart, the path down to the cavern finally visible. The group shook in terror as Nole was startled by the vibrations felt deep below, realising that it was finally time. The passage to Midnight Cavern was open.

When the group ventured towards the crack, the size of the entrance felt much larger up close. The crack, wide enough for the whole group to fall through, stood between what seemed to be the final challenge— the answer to Wandjina.

“Well, who's first?” Nash asked jokingly, knowing well Nole wanted him to go first. Nash waited tensely, not wanting to be the first in.

“I'll go,” Salem said confidently, easing his mech. Gently dipping his mech's front legs in, the rift swallowed Salem whole as he swiftly leapt forward, landing in a monotone tone cavern shaded with unusual flora. Thick grey vines stretched in all directions, reaching around every corner as if it was wrapping up a secret. “I'm alright\!”

Hesitating for a moment, Nole began his descent, lowering his mech into the cavern. Landing with a smooth thud, he brushed off the unsettling dust that had coated his mech from the impact. Nole had been to Midnight Cavern before, but even so, to him it felt like a stranger. The vines again, felt alive somehow. The ghastly grim grey had coloured the cavern sick. Following Nole's successful entrance, Iris was next, swiftly landing in silence.  

*Katamanyu Noluch…*

The voices rang again. Iris voided out, lost in a state where her mind entered some entity. It felt surreal, the connection strong and intact. Suddenly snapping back, Iris glanced around as if she had just seen a ghost. Her hand, steadily placed on her blaster, was ready— expecting something to pounce out from the shadows. But it was just a voice, a sweet, innocent voice of a mole that sounded brightly narcissistic.

“Ah good\! You're here Nole\! With some friends I see? I guess it will make this much more efficient\!” Shrue smiled, greeting the group.

But the chat was cut short with Nash's dramatic entrance, almost falling off his mech as he tumbled down. “Whoops…”

“Anyways…” Shrue continued, annoyed by the interruption. “We need to dismantle these boulders as the cavern extends this way…”

The group followed Shrue into a small room, finding two cave trolls slaved away to Shrue's command. They tugged on the boulders that were glued to the floor, barricading the tunnel that was visible through the gaps between them. 

“Should we try with slugs?” Nole suggested, reaching for his blaster. 

Shrue threw an aggressive expression, tempered with a tone of disgust. “Are you stupid Nole? This is such a small room. Our slugs will *not* reach velocimorph\! Hurry up and get going\!” he commanded.

“Woah, chill. But let's get moving. Let's see what this is about…” Nash interrupted, trying to calm the tension. As the intensity settled, the group joined in on assisting the cave trolls to move the boulders, slowly making progress. The harder they pushed, Iris could feel the vines shriek, gasping for air. For Salem, all he could think about was the dimness. It was still noir all around, with themselves being the only source of colour. Something just had felt wrong about the cavern. 

Tug after tug, action followed by action, the boulders eventually were separated, revealing the small tunnel further down the cavern. Nash stared blankly, hypnotized by the darkness it shrouded. 

The tunnel spat them out into a cave not that different from the previous room, slightly larger but where stones clutched on the ceiling, hanging down in strange shapes. Black vines sprawled out from what seemed to be the center catalyst— the well. Its branches crawled up the walls like ragged curtains, sheathing away the stony mess.

But the most bizarre of it all was at the center of the cavern, where the strange capsule-like device sat wedged into the throat of the well-like pit.

“This place gives me the creeps…” Nash whispered. 

Iris rolled her eyes, flattered. “C'mon Nash, what's the worst that can happen?”

As Nole overheard, he couldn't help but grin widely, smirking at the words. “Coward…” he muttered under his breath. A faint rumbling shook the cavern, but for once, nothing happened. Wandjina was powerless for some reason. The cloaking vines, almost as a net that held back Wandjina proved mystical, only making Salem more skeptical.

“Woah…what's in there?” Salem asked. He leant down, touching the capsule. Inside, Salem could make out a vial but not what was inside. It was a liquid of some sort, perhaps unstable given the protection that shielded it. “Will it really cure Wandjina?”

“Of course. We've been searching for decades. This is the pinnacle of medicine\!” Shrue exclaimed, trying to pull the capsule out.

“Unlike *someone*, who has explored for months has gotten nowhere\!” Nole emphasised, briefly glancing at Nash before his eyes locked back onto the two muscular trolls ready to unlodge the capsule.

Iris gazed with intrigue, feeling the archaic steel that encased the supposed cure. It was cold and firm to the touch, the shell almost breathing with a heavy burden. 

“Well let's get going\! What do we do now?” Nole exclaimed.

Shrue recalled his plan, initiating the sequence at the sound of his fist. “We will need to go to the Deep Roots and inject this into the heart. Afterall, it's the core of the Ridgelands\! With its vast network, it will be over\! The days of Wandjina— no, suffering will be over\!”

As the cave trolls hauled the capsule over their shoulders and carried it out, Iris lingered around for a little longer. The pit, now empty, formed what seemed to be a well, digging into the core of the Earth. Iris kicked the dust, knocking pebbles and rocks into the well to see what would happen.

“Well, let's get this over with. We've been side-tracked helping the two of you— Once this is over, are you guys willing to help us?” Salem smiled.

Shrue struck his hand at Salem's back as they exchanged looks. Shrue wore his ensuring smile, backing Salem up. “Of course, there is nothing that we will not deny. But first, let's go to the deep roots\!”

“No…This isn't right…” Nash churned under his breath. He flicked his rifle out, aiming for the capsule.

“Now, now Nash. This isn't the time to throw a tantrum,” Nole said in an inadvertent motherly tone. His hand rested on Nash's blaster, slowly putting it down. “C'mon, don't you hate Wandjina too?” 

The dilemma struck. It felt too surreal for the day to have finally come. For years, since birth, Nash disliked Wandjina for what it had become. As Iris watched Shrue and Nole exit first, followed by the cautious Salem taking his time, Iris turned back into the room with the well. 

“C'mon Iris, let's go ask about your father once this is settled…”

*My father? Why do I not remember anything?* 

“Iris are you alright?” Nash glanced back, worried. 

Iris's heart skipped a beat, time dilating to a snail speed. Her mind went blank before she finally rushed out a sentence. “No, I'm alright,” 

As she wandered back into the room, Nash followed closely, becoming even more concerned.

“Iris, we need to go\! What's gotten into your head? Don't tell me the Deep Roots never healed you fully…”

Suddenly, a brief flash of red rose from the hole, a nostalgic glint that reminded Iris of a deathly aura. Nash was blinded by the terror— Salem's sadness and Iris's rage reignited within him. For a moment, he was terrified. That same crimson essence, ensnaring those who came close was here again. Iris fell backwards, almost as if that something had rushed into her blood again, Nash barely able to catch her. Nash's eyes widened, already regretting his actions. He should have never trusted Shrue. Not even Nole. 

“No…It can't be…No…” But Nash knew it was true. Dark Water radiated bright red from the well, his greatest fears answered.
