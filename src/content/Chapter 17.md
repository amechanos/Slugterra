### The Ridgeland's Secret, Part 1

“Mech's ready\!” Raphael exclaimed as Iris and Salem returned to the store. Iris, delighted to see her mech back in pristine condition gently patted its head, saddling on to finally feel the long awaited comfort of her mech. It was polished like new, the body gleaming in the Solaryte. 

“Wow…” Iris gasped, admiring the renovations. The paint seemed to have been recoated and primed, casting a vibrant deep red. “This colour…”

Nash smiled. Raphael watched proudly, glad he was able to help more. Through helping Iris reclaim her mech, Raphael evoked an unseen force of joy into his spirit, something strange that he had never felt in the Ridgelands. The constant shift of terrain must have affected his emotions, already crumbling one's minds and fracturing connections. 

Gazzers and Diggrix slugs popped out from holes on the ground, peeping with curiosity. Nash just stood there, not making a sound but gently smiling. As Iris's chatter soothed the quiet store, the ground began to move. It was live, shifting beneath their feet. It was as if the Earth was listening to Iris's voice.  Unlike the sudden motions, the ground slid downwards, snapping itself neatly against a neighbouring chunk of ground. The path was aligned, almost as if it was guiding Iris on where to go next.

“Can’t thank you enough Raph,” Nash smirked, mounting his own mech. 

Raph signalled his goodbye, before returning to his store. “Stay safe kid\!”

“So, where are we off to?” Iris asked. Nash shrugged, also with no answer.

“I don't know. Solaryte crystals are depowering so I doubt we'll get anywhere in the dark. We'll head back towards Askent tomorrow,” Nash explained. As the two trudged back through the town square, it felt like Wandjina had calmed down. The pillars— they weren't excruciatingly sunken or raised, rather a reasonable distance vertically from one chunk to another. Something had changed. The statue they had passed earlier looked whole now— the head rolled back onto the top of its body, just not aligned correctly. 

Iris didn't dare to raise the topic of Wandjina even though a sneaking suspicion began to rise within her internally, starting to piece together something that felt personal and also ugly about the Ridgelands. It was just too quiet. Even the day before, Wandjina felt active every moment they were in the Ridgelands. And now, it felt calm for some reason.

“C'mon\!” Nash motioned as he parked his mech outside his home. The night was quiet to say the least, but the vibrations of Wandjina began to pick up again. Iris felt it. 

*Rise, Kura. Tjulmura ilunpa kanyil.*

\~

The elusive Chasm slug perched above the fungi, watching down through the fractured terrain. Salem, Nole and Shrue marched forwards along the rocky path. 

“Where are we headed?” Salem asked, trailing the pack. 

“I've got a few associates in Bassecreuse, so we're going to make a stop there,” Shrue explained.

“Bassecreuse? Oh come on\!” Nole frustratingly exclaimed.

“Why? What's wrong, Nole?” Salem said.

Nole slowed his pace down to come parallel to Salem's mech before he began aggressively gesturing out his anger. “It is a place that lacks technology\! The rest of those Ridgelanders think they are better than us\!” 

“I wouldn't say that Nole,” Shrue chuckled, parking his mech down next to the gate. Bassecreuse's southern entrance, currently set beneath a towering pillar opened up a small gate that led the group in, feeding into a small alley that slowly caved into what seemed to be the luxurious sprawl of living. Vibrancy and saturated greens highlighted with the occasionally contrasting signs was enough to intrigue Salem to the more grandeur, classical vibed city than what he had seen earlier with Verdud.

“This is Bassecreuse?” Salem gasped in awe, absolutely intrigued by the fascinating buildings that embedded themselves into the rocky pillars. 

Nole rolled his eyes, almost expecting Nole to appreciate Bassecreuse over Verdud. It wasn't surprising to him, but he was slightly disappointed. “Yes, yes. Just don't get lost okay? We're going to wait for Shrue.”

Before Salem knew it, Shrue was already gone. He had just seen him standing beside Nole a moment ago, but now, Shrue had faded into the city, tucking away into the cracks of the towering city. Salem could feel the city calling to him, eager to just wander around and let Wandjina carry him through the pillars.

“Hey\! Don't wander too far okay?” Nole reminded. Amidst the quiet square, Salem minded his own business and walked through the metaphorical mist that shrouded the city with mystery. It felt like a city of promise, with something destined to cross his path.

Almost immediately, a voice caught Salem’s attention. As he proceeded to turn the corner, he found the main street shifting upwards, giving him a clear view of the city beneath him. And there, in the crowd like fish in the sea, Salem spotted her. Iris was there. 

Salem ran like a kid in an amusement park, beelining towards Iris. “Iris\!”

“Woah, Salem\! I didn’t expect you to be here\! How’d you know we were here?” Iris grinned happily, seeing Salem after a long while. With a deep exchange of a heartfelt hug, the sudden tension broke and for a moment, the crowd seemed to dissipate.

“Well, it’s a long story. Glad to see you’re doing fine,” Salem said, smiling awkwardly. “What are you doing?”

“Well me and Nash were originally going to search for you and Nole, but since you’re here, we’ve basically got nothing to do,” 

“Well, you could help me. Nole tells me we can stop Wandjina and potentially make the lives of many people better…” Salem suggested, motioning Iris to follow her. 

“Say what?” Nash interrupted, walking in on the conversation. “Ah, Salem\! We were about to go looking for you and Nole\! What have you guys been up to?”

Salem paused for a moment. Something just didn't feel right, but he continued his story as usual. “I was just telling Iris about how Nole took me around Verdud, his home town. Feels so different to Bassacreuse…It's like living in two different time periods…”

“Salem\! There you are— Oh\! What a surprise\!" Nole exclaimed as he found the group gathered near the square. “What is happening here?”

“Hey Nole, been a minute?” Nash smiled. Iris grinned at Nole's arrival while Salem looked at him staunchly. Nole raised an eyebrow, confused.

“Say, why don't we grab some food?” Nash suggested. “I know a good spot— I know this might be a bad time, but y'all just gotta trust me\!” 

Nole grinned ear to ear, happily following Nash as he pushed against the archaic wooden door, revealing the energetic interior. Music blasted as the crowd chanted, where jugs of beer and dishes flooded the tables. It was almost hypnotising for the party to understand what was going on— except for Nash who only wandered to an empty round table, sitting down before the others followed. The warm lights overhead only amplified the mood, where the group blankly stared at each other.

“Hey boss, 5 please\! And one Lasseirue\!” Nash exclaimed. 

“We shouldn't take too long, Nash…” Salem muttered, with a worried tone. “Nole and Shrue have a plan to eliminate Wandjina and make the Ridgelands safer…”

“Just take it easy Salem, have some fun\! Don't worry about it\!” Nash laughed, toasting with pleasure. 

“I guess…” Salem muttered, rustling his hair back, almost somewhat embarrassed to be in such a place. The bar only burned on with gold and crimson light. Every surface was drenched in a haze that turned ordinary moments into fragments of a fever dream. Music thundered through the room, a relentless heartbeat pounding beneath the roar of conversation. Glasses collided in sparkling flashes, where scattering droplets caught the light like liquid diamonds before vanishing into the crowd. The air slowly indulged into a concentrated scent of citrus and whiskey, sharp and sweet on the tongue, while the rich scent of charred meat, perfume, and spilt beer drifted through the heat. Salem almost felt sick, while Iris powered on, enjoying the Lasseirue.

The afternoon went by like a slick burst of a summer's breeze. The room blurred into streaks of colour and motion, yet every sensation remained vivid. The central pot of goodness was already devoured by the hungry. Iris, Nash and Nole laid in their chairs while Salem had still half a bowl of food sitting in front of him.

“C'mon, cheer up\! What is your problem Salem?” Nole joked.

Salem slouched forwards in his chair, collapsing his elbows on the table with a heavy sigh. “Aren't you forgetting something? Isn't Shrue waiting for us?”

“I already told him\!” Nole assured, smiling again. But there was still a blatant suspicion laying in Salem's opinion and it wouldn't change. Having Iris still be carefree and adapting to the Ridgelands rather quickly had only heighted his belief.

“I'm going for some air,” Salem exhaled, removing himself from the bar. The crisp, fresh air quickly overtook his senses as he stepped outside, the blinding lights of the Solaryte crystals quickly coming into effect. Salem cast his hand over his eyes to block the scorching rays before he looked around. Bassacreuse was quiet. It wasn't like this before. The ground was even and flush, no extruding pillars. In the distance, another pillar fell, sinking into the Earth.

As he turned back, ready to re-enter the tavern, the ground had shifted. The building had now hung atop the pillar that shot up like a finger, with almost no way for Salem to return. 

“Ah, fuck,” Salem cursed, trying to find a way back. 

\~

As the group climbed down the stairs descending the pillar the tavern stood upon, Iris found Salem sitting near the base of the pillar.

"Hey, what's wrong?" Iris asked, genuinely concerned.

"No. This feels so weird. Nole is right. We need to find a way to remove Wandjina..." Salem exclaimed.

Iris frowned. "Because you got stuck on a pillar?"

"No, because this keeps happening\!" Salem snapped. "People have to plan their lives around it. Verdud gets torn apart every day. Everyone acts like it's normal."

"It is normal," Nash shrugged.

"That's exactly my point\!" Salem shot back. "Why is everybody okay with this?"

Nole crossed his arms. "Now you're starting to understand."

Salem pointed towards the shifting city. "If Shrue says we might have a chance at stopping Wandjina, why wouldn't we help him?"

For a moment, nobody answered.

Iris looked away towards the quiet streets of Bassacreuse. The ground here was calm. The pillars sat almost perfectly aligned.

"I don't know," she admitted. "I mean, what's wrong with trying? What's the worst that can happen, Nash?"

Nash glanced back, only with confusion in his expression. “Yeah…I don't know. Salem you sure you're fine? Did you get brainwashed by those moles in Verdud?" 

Nash's joke of a question was all that it needed for another pillar to come rushing out like a needle, lifting the group high into the sky. Nole didn't seem to even bother with Nash's jokes, almost as if he was already used to them.

“Anyways Nash, if you're going to help, we're going to *Midnight Cavern*,” Nole said as he rolled his eyes. His expression of the location felt rather slurred, almost giving the cavern a deadly tone to it. 

“Midnight Cavern? Like from that tale of legend where it's open only for 4 hours a day?” Nash replied with a tensed tone. Panic was subtle in his eyes, almost as if he was afraid to go there. Nole nodded firmly, the cause already in action.

Before the group knew it, they were off, moving back out of the populated town of Bassacreuse into the unknown Ridgelands.
