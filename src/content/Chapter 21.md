### The Dreaming, Part 3

“How long do we have?” Iris panicked, fear ensnaring her voice.

“I don't know. I'm assuming Shrue needs to open up the Deep Root's core. If he hasn't opened it yet, it will take at least another 10 minutes\!” Nash estimated. He coughed, still holding his wounded arm.

“It's alright Nash, me and Iris will finish this,” Salem said, holding his hand out. Nash sat confused, but Iris grasped it, understanding the internal pain. “I wasn't able to save my home, and I'd hate to see yours fall too. C'mon Iris, it's the least we can do.”

“I guess I'll find you when it's all over…” Iris smiled weakly before she jumped back on her mech.

Salem led the way as the two rode on their mechs, diving into the depths of the Deep Roots. The ground gave way. The deep vibrations began to feel its way into their mechs, Iris feeling lightheaded. The familiar essence of the Deep Roots crept into Iris's body, the faint voices of the spirits returning. For once, it was louder than ever. The source was the Deep Roots and this time, Iris couldn't mistake it.

*Hurry. The energy that connects the land will be broken. The Ridgelands do not stand another day.*

As they neared the central roots, the grunts of a cave troll grew louder. Iris patiently approached the entrance, the core's vibrant aquamarine glow reaching the pathway.

In the darkness, the cave troll launched forwards, tackling Iris off her mech as she stepped foot into the shadows. The cave troll smashed down with authority before Iris could react. But before the cave troll could land a sledgehammer to her face, the Chasm reacted, teleporting her meters away to safety. Iris gasped for air before she stood up, holding her blaster firmly.

“Don't underestimate the moles\!” the cave troll yelled, pulling out a bulky machine gun loaded with Hoprocks. Salem's eyes widened as Iris froze at the sight of the abomination, knowing that they were outmatched.

The flurry of Hoprocks barraged inside the cave, widening the area significantly. Light from the core flooded outwards as Salem spotted Shrue carefully dissecting the branches that covered the core. 

“Rahhhh\!” the cave troll shouted, unloading the entire rack of Hoprocks. Iris kept dodging, but kept constantly falling and getting scratched by the flying debris while Salem tried counterplay. His slugs didn't stand a chance.

“Salem\! Look\! The vial's on his strap\!” Iris exclaimed, trying to reach him. The dust didn't settle— it lingered like a heavy, permanent fog that blinded the entire cavern. But it wasn't over. The cave troll whistled, the Hop Rocks quickly returning to him as he prepared his second round. 

“Where are you traitors? The ones who want to destroy their own home? Huh? Come out, cowards\!” the cave troll yelled. His voice, still holding a demonic voice, echoed loudly as Iris and Salem tried to fan the dust away.

“Here…” the voice came from a different direction. The cave troll's eyes darted left and right, but his own creation of dust had blinded his vision, the voice unlocatable. Nash's finger instinctively pressed the trigger on his blaster, aiming his Speedstinger without thought. His heart steered him right, his trusty slug hitting the cave troll’s strap. The vial popped upwards, into the air for anyone's taking.

“Nash\!” Iris exclaimed, watching Nash force himself into the fight. Somehow, he had crawled his way on his mech to the depths of the Deep Roots, retracting his rifle back into a blaster form. Grasping it in his left hand, Iris felt his presence enchanting, the tone of the battle shifting significantly.

“Don't…worry about me,” Nash coughed. “We’re doing this together…right?”

The Cave Troll, reacting fast, quickly jumped up, reaching for the vial. Salem barged through the fog, firing his Slicksilver, pulling the machine gun away. Nash's Speedstinger, still active, ricocheted off the right wall, displacing the vial yet again in the air like magic. The cave troll tumbled to the ground before Nash placed the nail in the coffin. When he slung an Arachnet he had picked up earlier, he wrapped the cave troll up in webs, completely taking him out of the battle.

The three both rushed towards where the vial was, still freefalling. But it was too late. Salem froze when he noticed that Shrue was nowhere to be found. The core stood exposed, the fate of the Ridgelands minutes away from collapse.

“Where's Shrue?” Nash yelled as he sprinted towards the others. Suddenly, Shrue leapt from the finally settling dust, stepping on Nash as he jumped for the vial. “Argh\!”

Nash collapsed forwards, holding his injured arm tight as he curled up in pain. Iris cried out, expression filled with hatred. The ground shook, Salem feeling the growing vibrations. As she aggressively tugged on Shrue as he was airborne, the vial fell as it finally landed on the ground, rolling away towards the core. The two were grounded, with the race to the vial being inevitable.

Iris dove forwards, reaching for the vial while Shrue scrambled towards it. Suddenly, the cave troll roared, breaking free from his webs and grabbing his regular blaster. He fired his shot, a hoprock aimed at Iris. As the slug inched closer, Iris rolled away, barely able to dodge under the slug's impact with the ground. Shrue snatched the vial with ease and all Iris could do still laying on the ground was watch. 

Her heartbeat was spiking, gasping for a breath as she finally collected herself. Her body was badly bruised, finally standing back up as she watches Shrue hold the vial like a newborn baby.

“No\! Do you not know what this can do to the Ridgelands?” Iris begged. “The Ridgelands— you've been told a lie\!”

“A lie? When it is actively trying to imprison us? Us moles are the epitome of knowledge. If someone is wrong, it's *you.*” Shrue coldly stated. Iris scrambled to her feet, but Shrue taunted Iris. His fingers wrapped around the vial's cap, ready to unscrew it and drench the core. 

“This isn't what the Ridgelands is…” Iris muttered, her fists clenched tight. As he unscrewed the cap, Iris felt darkness, time slowing down. 

"Look around you\!" Shrue gestured towards the fractured cavern. "Collapsed tunnels. Broken homes. Wandjina twists our world every day and you defend it? You call it balance. I call it suffering. For the first time in history we have the chance to control our future.”

“Is it worth it…?” Salem argued. “This place…it's no different than my home…”

“Well, this isn't your home\!” Shrue snapped. But in that moment, something clicked inside her mind. The messages, voices all stopped. It all merged into something strange, a feeling she couldn't describe. The faint mindscape— that aquamarine plane she had been in reemerged, but not as the same pedestal she had been at, but an overlay to the environment around her. Before she knew it, her mind raced. The Chasm slug from her belt acted instinctively, just like her thoughts. It teleported onto her blaster first, then teleported her blaster into her hand that already extended outwards, almost as if she knew her blaster was already there. The Chasm teleported itself into the chamber as Iris slammed the trigger down.

Shrue's hand had tipped the vial over, but it wasn't enough for the dark water to spill. The Chasm won. The blinding flash of blue transformed, snatching Shrue's dreams away. As it teleported the vial away, the sheer physics of the universe broke, the vial shattering as the Chasm found itself deep within the ravines of the Ridgelands that tunnelled into the Deep Caverns. Glass fell like rain while the Dark Water formed a blob. The Chasm returned to protoform, catching its breath before it finished the job. But the speed it took the Chasm to rereach velocimorph was slow, the Dark Water fast enough to reach and drench the Chasm, ghouling itself as it freefell down back into the velocimorph. 

Meanwhile, the fight was over. The Ridgelands were saved. The Dark Water was gone. Shrue stood motionless, frozen at the sight of his research disappearing in an instant.

“Oh my god…” Iris gasped as she saw Shrue, empty handed at the core. They had done it. If even one drop had touched the core, it would have been over. The miraculous sight sent a wave of relief into Nash's chest while Iris collapsed to her knees in exhaustion. Nash cheered while Salem celebrated. Shrue was speechless while the cave troll trudged over angrily.

“You said you could fix Wandjina with that\!” the furious cave troll yelled. “You betrayed us…”

The cave troll had no remorse, a tank fueled by pure anger. He loaded his blaster with a rare Geoshard, intending to impale Shrue dead. He pounced towards Shrue, his finger on the trigger. The shell fell as the slug raced towards Shrue's heart, but a flash of crimson saved his life. The Geoshard disappeared.

Complete silence.

Then, the Cave Troll's arm was ripped off. Gone. It didn't fall to the ground, rather completely dismembered from his body as he screamed in pain. Blood trickled down as Shrue, Iris, Salem and Nash all watched the grotesque scene in horror. Before his screams could complete, the rest of his body was ground, the only remains of him being the thick pool of red blood on the ground. Iris’s jaw hung open, horrified by the sight.

Shrue, Iris, Nash and Salem all stared blankly in silence.

The cavern was dead silent.

Not a single word.

“Iris—” Nash blurted out. “What—”

“No…Don’t come near me…” Iris groaned weakly.

Salem inched closer, his hand out. “Iris?”

Then it ripped again, the crimson wings emerging from nothing. For a moment, Nash and Salem saw the monster's form, a deranged minotaur like slug summoned out of thin air. 

“Iris\! Look out\!” Nash yelled. But the pain in his arm made him flinch. He couldn't force himself forwards to save Iris as the red abomination collided with her, placing her fate as the same as the cave troll's. She had been teleported to some place in the unknown.

“Iris\!” Salem yelled, trying to catch the strands of red before it finally dissipated. Silence engulfed the cavern for a moment when the crimson finally blipped out of existence again along with Iris, giving Nash, Salem and Shrue nightmare fuel that began to haunt them immediately. 

Salem’s breathing hastened while Nash’s heartbeat galloped loudly, trying to leave his chest. It felt far from over. Shrue scrambled to the corner, terrified of his own fate. Nobody dared to fight. For Nash and Salem, fighting wasn't even on their minds. The priority of beating Shrue had fallen. All they wanted was to know where Iris went. To save her. Nothing else.

“Iris?” the two whispered in harmony. No response. She was gone. 

Shrue's eyes lit up as if he just had an eureka moment. And then it happened again. The crimson slug appeared, but this time it fell back into protoform. 

When Nash could finally see it still for a second, he saw the unimaginable. The Chasm slug rolled unconscious onto the ground in front of Shrue, embroidered with a black band around its body. Its eyes and body glowed a deep velvet instead of its original magenta, radiating the unmatched and familiar dark energy. The Spasm had arrived. 

“You…What are you? Such power…” Shrue muttered. “How do you dislocate like that?” 

Shrue stared at the slug. For the first time, he looked afraid. The gruesome sequence felt too strong. Even though it was unconscious, Shrue was cautious around it, terrified his arm was going to be ripped off. But at the thought of tearing things apart, a new idea to break Wandjina rose in his mind. Fear had become fascination. With a quick hand, Shrue captured it in a shell, fueling his own goals.

“If this is the last thing I do, I will save the Ridgelands\!”

“No…” Nash panicked. He didn't dare fight. If that Spasm was Shrue's weapon, nobody stood a chance after seeing what it did to Iris and the cave troll. Where were they? The void? The Deep Caverns? Or a limbo in an unknown place? Nothing felt real. Salem and Nash could sense each other's panic, both evident in their eyes and body. As Shrue strolled out, Spasm loaded in his blaster, Nash and Salem could only remain still.

But the only mutual thought was still Iris. They  scrambled to find Iris. But of course, the attempt was futile. They knew deep inside they had lost their greatest asset. 

“No…This…can't be…” Salem whimpered. His heart ached, knowing now he had failed her many times. Nash, still in pain, weakly stood beside him, trying to comfort him. It was over. They had to start from nothing. 

Though still frightful, the two remained quiet for a moment. “Salem…” Nash began. 

“What?”

“I think I’ve cracked it…It all makes sense…”

“What does?” Salem responded, eager to know what Nash had plotted.

“Wandjina. It’s more complex than anything. I don’t think it’s pure Slug Energy…”

“Slow down Nash\! What do you mean, isn’t Wandjina a god?” Salem argued, still brainwashed by the moles’ propaganda.

“It’s the truth…It’s almost like Wandjina mirrors our relationships— the moment Iris disappeared, my ties with Nole, the vibrations only felt stronger…” Nash slumped forwards, his grasp on his blaster failing. Clutching his right arm, Salem rushed beside him, helping him up.

“It’s okay Nash, we need to take things slow. I know it’s hard to process everything, but I can assure you we’ll be alright. Let’s just get out of here, eh?”

Elsewhere, fate had decided the unfortunate ends of ones. The cave troll? Dropped into the endless ravines down into the Deep Caverns. They were likely dead. But for Iris, she was more fortunate. Her eyes opened to a blue ceiling, her body encased in a shallow pond. The bed was rather muddy as she finally sat up, only to find herself in a completely new place.
